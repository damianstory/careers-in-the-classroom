Use it in class

Ask the class

If a sensor detects no methane, does that prove none is present? What would you want to know about the instrument and the observation conditions?

Detecting a gas, estimating how much is escaping, and deciding what to do are different tasks. Instruments, analysis and the people interpreting results all contribute.

Mindset it shows

Understand measurement limits before drawing conclusions.

The lesson, in the real world

The physics of light lets a satellite about 500 km up measure a gas no one can see.

  1. 1

    Sunlight contains many wavelengths, including shortwave infrared light our eyes can’t see.

  2. 2

    Methane absorbs some of that light at particular wavelengths, between about 1,630 and 1,675 nanometres.

  3. 3

    The satellite’s spectrometer measures sunlight scattered back up from the ground. It sends out no light of its own.

  4. 4

    Where methane is present, a little less light arrives at those wavelengths. Analysts combine that dip with wind data to estimate how fast gas is escaping.
