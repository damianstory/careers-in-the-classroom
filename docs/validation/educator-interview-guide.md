# Educator validation interview

Draft, 24 September 2026. Proposed 25–30 minutes. Recruit 5–6 Calgary-area educators across biology, chemistry, physics, and integrated/applied science through existing introductions. No recruitment has been sent.

## Before showing the idea — 7 minutes

1. What courses are you teaching, and what topic are you working on now?
2. Tell me about the last time you connected a lesson to a real organization or career. What did you use and how did you find it?
3. What did students do with that example? What, if anything, was difficult?
4. Have you tried to bring a practitioner into class? Walk me through what happened.

Ask for concrete past examples; do not assume the problem is lack of time or information.

## Use a sample — 10 minutes

Give them the closest matching example from the pack. Say: “This is a researched draft. Please show me what you would use, change, or ignore.”

5. Where could this fit in an actual lesson? If it does not fit, why?
6. Which claims would you check before sharing? What information is missing?
7. What would you do next to put it in front of students? Observe their preferred method before offering slides, printable material, or a link.
8. Is the local connection useful here? Would a better non-local example be more useful?
9. What other topic would you actually want to search for?

Record whether an example is relevant, merely interesting, or usable with specific changes. Do not equate enthusiasm with use.

## Test the service choices — 7 minutes

10. Explain one full free preview followed by registration for additional results. Ask where they would stop. Show the proposed fields: email (school email encouraged), school, board/authority, courses. Ask which are difficult or unnecessary; do not collect details without an actual reason.
11. Show the two news samples after the evergreen example. Which would be useful to share, and what makes it credible or too speculative?
12. Describe a monthly email of relevant examples and opportunities. What would make it worth opening? How should they change topic when the class moves on?
13. Show “Request a speaker.” What would they expect to happen, how far ahead would they ask, and what would they need before committing?

## Close with a behavioral next step — 3 minutes

14. Is there a specific upcoming lesson where you want to try one example? Agree a follow-up only if they choose to try it.
15. What would make this unnecessary because something you already use does the job better?

For willing participants, follow up after the chosen lesson: Was it used? What did they show or say? What questions did students ask? What preparation was needed? Do they want another example? Record self-report separately from materials or other evidence they voluntarily share. No student identifying information is needed.

## Interview record

Record course/topic, current workaround, chosen example, exact objections, required edits, preferred classroom format, signup reaction, digest reaction, speaker expectation, agreed next action, and actual follow-up outcome. Keep interview notes separate from source research.

## Proposed decision rule

Use this small pilot to identify patterns, not statistical proof. A promising signal would be at least three educators using a sample and at least two asking for another topic or taking a concrete relationship step. Report classroom use and TKS relationship outcomes separately. If feedback is positive but nobody uses anything, investigate fit and delivery before expanding the catalogue.
