## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A maintenance decision needs evidence, not a guess.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. An aircraft component must meet requirements appropriate to its purpose and operating conditions.

2. Avmax lists inspections and non-destructive testing among its maintenance capabilities. These can provide evidence without simply breaking a part to examine it.

3. Technicians compare findings with applicable procedures and limits, involving the qualified people needed for the decision.

4. The resulting work and tests must be recorded. A part looking intact is not the same as having enough evidence to return it to service.

**Discuss:** What would you do if a measurement fell very close to an acceptance limit?

**Try thinking it through:** Give students fictional inspection readings, measurement uncertainty and a fictional acceptance interval. Ask which cases need more evidence. Do not use real aircraft limits.

**Teacher note:** This is a reasoning exercise, not aircraft-maintenance guidance. The response should distinguish a measured value from confidence in that value.

**Subject connection:** Physics: measurement, forces and material behaviour. Course mapping remains provisional.

**Evidence distinction:** Company process statements come from the sources above. The teaching interpretation, fictional activity and discussion are editorial. No fictional values are company measurements.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Aircraft maintenance

Investigate condition, carry out authorized maintenance and preserve the records behind a decision. Aircraft maintenance work relies on approved procedures and appropriate qualifications.

Typical tasks:

- Inspect a component using the applicable procedure.
- Compare findings with documented limits and escalate uncertainty.
- Record work and test results for the maintenance decision.

**Works with:** Licensed AMEs, avionics specialists, engineering and quality teams.

**Classroom connection:** A visual impression alone is not enough evidence that a part is safe.

**Company evidence:** Current MRO page names technicians, maintenance checks and inspection services.

### Avionics and electronics

Work on the electronic systems that allow aircraft to communicate, navigate and collect data. Troubleshooting means distinguishing a faulty sensor from wiring, power or software problems.

Typical tasks:

- Trace a signal through connected components.
- Use test equipment to compare expected and measured behaviour.
- Document the fault and verify the result after authorized work.

**Works with:** Aircraft maintenance teams, electrical designers and software developers.

**Classroom connection:** Circuits and signals become useful when the complete system works reliably.

**Company evidence:** Current company contact and service pages establish Calgary avionics work.

### Quality and traceability

Make it possible to explain how a result or product was produced. Quality work checks records, methods and acceptance criteria so variation can be investigated rather than hidden.

Typical tasks:

- Check that materials and samples have traceable identities.
- Compare recorded results with defined acceptance criteria.
- Investigate deviations and document what changed.

**Works with:** Production, laboratory, engineering and customer teams.

**Classroom connection:** Repeated results need both measurements and records of the conditions that produced them.

**Company evidence:** Inspection and documented maintenance provide the context; the role explanation is general.
