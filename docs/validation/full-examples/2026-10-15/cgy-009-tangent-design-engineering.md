## Company tab — proposed copy

Tangent Design Engineering is a Calgary engineering and design consultancy. Its Katal case study describes designing and building a transportable demonstration skid for a refining process. Mechanical, electrical and process engineers worked with in-house millwrights to develop a controllable prototype that could fit in a shipping container.

**The problem:** What has to change when a laboratory process becomes a transportable system?

**What it offers:** Engineering design, prototyping and integration services.

**Who uses the work:** Teams needing to test or demonstrate a technology beyond laboratory scale.

**Evidence:** [Katal project and Calgary shop](https://www.tangentservices.com/projects/katal)

## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A working laboratory process still needs a design for the field.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. Katal had demonstrated a process in the laboratory but needed a larger system for testing and demonstration.

2. Tangent’s case study describes using process-flow and piping-and-instrumentation diagrams to plan the equipment and controls.

3. A multidisciplinary team designed and assembled a transportable skid, accounting for control, safety and packaging constraints.

4. The prototype provided a platform for further testing. Building a demonstration does not by itself establish commercial performance or environmental benefit.

**Discuss:** Why might multiplying every dimension of a laboratory setup by ten fail to produce a workable plant?

**Try thinking it through:** Ask students to fit a fictional process into a fixed rectangular footprint while leaving access for maintenance and showing measurement points. Compare designs against the same constraints.

**Teacher note:** Focus on relationships and access, not engineering specifications. The exercise is not a plan for handling hazardous materials or building the Katal process.

**Subject connection:** Physics: scaling, flow and measurement; Chemistry: process conditions. Formal curriculum fit remains provisional.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Process engineering

Turn a useful transformation into a workable sequence of equipment and operating steps. Process engineers consider what enters, what leaves and what conditions the system must maintain.

Typical tasks:

- Draw a flow of materials and energy through the system.
- Compare operating conditions and equipment options.
- Use trial results to revise the process and communicate constraints.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, operators, mechanical designers and controls specialists.

**Classroom connection:** Conservation of matter and energy connects classroom calculations to process decisions.

**Company evidence:** The case names process engineers and flow-diagram work.

### Instrumentation and controls

Keep a process measurable and responsive. Controls work connects sensors, decisions and actuators so the system can respond when conditions change.

Typical tasks:

- Check whether sensors are reading reliably.
- Compare measurements with the intended operating range.
- Test how the system responds to a change or fault.

**Works with:** Operators, electricians and process engineers.

**Classroom connection:** Feedback uses a measured result to inform the next action.

**Company evidence:** Electrical engineering and fine process control are described; role label is editorial.

### Industrial machinery assembly and maintenance

Make machinery work together as a physical system. Millwright work connects assembly, alignment, inspection and troubleshooting.

Typical tasks:

- Read equipment drawings and plan assembly steps.
- Align and connect mechanical components.
- Check operation and record problems that need adjustment.

**Works with:** Designers, operators, electricians and fabrication teams.

**Classroom connection:** Small alignment errors can change how forces and motion pass through a machine.

**Company evidence:** The case explicitly identifies in-house millwrights and shop assembly.
