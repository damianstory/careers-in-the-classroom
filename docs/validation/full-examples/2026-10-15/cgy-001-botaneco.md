## Company tab — proposed copy

Botaneco develops ingredients from oilseeds for feed, food and personal-care products. It describes a water-based process that separates proteins and oleosomes, the seed’s natural oil-containing structures. Its Calgary headquarters includes research and development staff; its team names biochemists, engineers, food scientists and technicians.

**The problem:** How do you separate useful components while retaining the properties a customer needs?

**What it offers:** Oilseed ingredients and a processing approach designed to preserve their functionality.

**Who uses the work:** Ingredient users developing aquaculture feeds, plant-based foods and personal-care products.

**Evidence:** [Company and Calgary team](https://botaneco.com/about/); [Processing platform](https://botaneco.com/technology/); [Products and uses](https://botaneco.com/); [Careers check](https://botaneco.com/careers/)

## Classroom tab — proposed copy

Useful ingredients are hidden inside an ordinary seed.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. A seed contains several kinds of material. Botaneco works with oilseeds including canola and sunflower.

2. The company describes an aqueous process for separating proteins and intact oleosomes while avoiding harsh processing conditions.

3. Different recovered components can serve different ingredient uses. Separating them is only part of the challenge; their behaviour in a product also matters.

4. A useful evaluation compares the amount recovered, the properties retained and the resources used. A company’s sustainability claim needs its own supporting evidence.

**Discuss:** If a process recovers more material but changes how it behaves, has it improved?

**Try thinking it through:** Give groups three fictional ingredient samples: one has high recovery, one stays mixed longer, and one needs less processing energy. Ask each group to choose a sample for a product and identify the missing test.

**Teacher note:** There is no universal winner. Students should state the product requirement before comparing evidence. Do not treat the fictional samples as Botaneco results.

**Subject connection:** Chemistry: mixtures and properties; Biology: molecules in living systems. Specific course/outcome mapping remains provisional.

**Evidence distinction:** Company process statements come from the sources above. The teaching interpretation, fictional activity and discussion are editorial. No fictional values are company measurements.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Ingredient and formulation science

Investigate how ingredients behave in a product. This work connects a material’s composition to properties such as texture, stability or performance in a mixture.

Typical tasks:

- Choose a property to compare across formulations.
- Prepare controlled trials and observe changes over time.
- Explain the trade-offs between ingredients and processing conditions.

**Works with:** Biochemists, laboratory technicians and product-development teams.

**Classroom connection:** Molecules and mixtures help explain why two ingredients can behave differently.

**Company evidence:** Botaneco explicitly identifies food scientists and biochemists; the task explanation is general.

### Laboratory analysis

Make measurements that other people can trust. A laboratory worker prepares samples, uses a method consistently and records enough detail for someone else to interpret the result.

Typical tasks:

- Prepare and label samples so their identity is preserved.
- Run comparisons or reference samples to check the method.
- Record results, units and limitations before sharing findings.

**Works with:** Scientists, process engineers and quality teams.

**Classroom connection:** A repeatable method and a fair comparison matter as much as a single impressive number.

**Company evidence:** The company names technicians; sample-analysis tasks are an editorial explanation, not a verified job description.

### Process engineering

Turn a useful transformation into a workable sequence of equipment and operating steps. Process engineers consider what enters, what leaves and what conditions the system must maintain.

Typical tasks:

- Draw a flow of materials and energy through the system.
- Compare operating conditions and equipment options.
- Use trial results to revise the process and communicate constraints.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, operators, mechanical designers and controls specialists.

**Classroom connection:** Conservation of matter and energy connects classroom calculations to process decisions.

**Company evidence:** The company names engineers and describes a processing platform; no specific vacancy is asserted.
