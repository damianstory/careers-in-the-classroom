## Classroom tab — proposed copy

Knowing what to make is different from making it consistently.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. A project begins with a defined biological material and requirements for its intended use.

2. Northern RNA describes synthesis or production, purification and analytical testing as connected services.

3. Testing and records allow a team to compare batches and investigate differences. Traceability connects a result to how the material was made.

4. A batch meeting manufacturing requirements does not establish that a medicine is effective. Product quality and clinical outcomes answer different questions.

**Discuss:** If two batches have the same amount of material, what other properties might you need to compare?

**Try thinking it through:** Use fictional batch records with different purity measurements and missing process notes. Ask which records would allow a meaningful comparison and what information is missing.

**Teacher note:** The records are invented. Students should distinguish amount, identity, purity and traceability without treating them as clinical results.

**Subject connection:** Biology: DNA/RNA and biological processes; Chemistry: purity and analytical measurement. Outcome mapping remains provisional.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Bioprocess development

Develop repeatable ways to make and purify biological materials. This work connects molecular science to manufacturing conditions and checks whether a change improves the process.

Typical tasks:

- Define a comparison between process conditions.
- Track production and purification steps for each batch.
- Use analytical results to decide which change to test next.

**Works with:** Molecular scientists, analytical staff and quality teams.

**Classroom connection:** Knowing a sequence or molecule is different from producing a reliable batch of it.

**Company evidence:** Company development and manufacturing pages describe process optimization and nucleic-acid production.

### Laboratory analysis

Make measurements that other people can trust. A laboratory worker prepares samples, uses a method consistently and records enough detail for someone else to interpret the result.

Typical tasks:

- Prepare and label samples so their identity is preserved.
- Run comparisons or reference samples to check the method.
- Record results, units and limitations before sharing findings.

**Works with:** Scientists, process engineers and quality teams.

**Classroom connection:** A repeatable method and a fair comparison matter as much as a single impressive number.

**Company evidence:** Analytical testing is a stated service.

### Quality and traceability

Make it possible to explain how a result or product was produced. Quality work checks records, methods and acceptance criteria so variation can be investigated rather than hidden.

Typical tasks:

- Check that materials and samples have traceable identities.
- Compare recorded results with defined acceptance criteria.
- Investigate deviations and document what changed.

**Works with:** Production, laboratory, engineering and customer teams.

**Classroom connection:** Repeated results need both measurements and records of the conditions that produced them.

**Company evidence:** The company explicitly identifies quality assurance working with manufacturing and lot traceability.
