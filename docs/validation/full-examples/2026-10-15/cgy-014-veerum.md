## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A digital view is useful only if it represents the place reliably.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. A physical site can be captured in measurements and images, while design models describe intended structures.

2. VEERUM describes combining visual datasets, including point clouds and textured 3D models, with additional asset information.

3. To compare them meaningfully, users need compatible location references and an understanding of when the information was captured.

4. A detailed model may still omit an object, contain an alignment error or be out of date. The decision determines how much confidence is needed.

**Discuss:** What would you check before using a digital model to decide whether new equipment will fit?

**Try thinking it through:** Give students two fictional site drawings: one is precisely scaled but old, and one is recent but approximate. Ask which decisions each supports and what new measurement would resolve the gap.

**Teacher note:** Accuracy, completeness and freshness are different qualities. More realistic rendering does not guarantee a better measurement.

**Subject connection:** Physics/applied mathematics: measurement, scale and coordinates; technology: data quality. Course fit is a review item.

**Evidence distinction:** Company process statements come from the sources above. The teaching interpretation, fictional activity and discussion are editorial. No fictional values are company measurements.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Spatial data and mapping

Turn measurements tied to places into usable spatial information. The work involves coordinates, scale and checking that datasets describe the same location and time.

Typical tasks:

- Check coordinate systems and measurement dates.
- Align and inspect spatial datasets.
- Present maps or models with their limitations.

**Works with:** Surveyors, engineers, field operators and software teams.

**Classroom connection:** Models can be detailed while still containing gaps or misaligned measurements.

**Company evidence:** Point-cloud and spatial visualization are documented capabilities; this does not prove VEERUM employs surveyors for every project.

### Software and data systems

Build the tools that move, process and display information. Reliable software needs testing against missing, delayed and unexpected data as well as ideal examples.

Typical tasks:

- Translate a user need into a testable behaviour.
- Build and test a data-processing or display feature.
- Investigate failures and document assumptions about inputs.

**Works with:** Domain experts, designers, users and hardware teams.

**Classroom connection:** An algorithm’s output depends on the quality and meaning of its inputs.

**Company evidence:** The product and careers materials support software work areas.

### Quality and traceability

Make it possible to explain how a result or product was produced. Quality work checks records, methods and acceptance criteria so variation can be investigated rather than hidden.

Typical tasks:

- Check that materials and samples have traceable identities.
- Compare recorded results with defined acceptance criteria.
- Investigate deviations and document what changed.

**Works with:** Production, laboratory, engineering and customer teams.

**Classroom connection:** Repeated results need both measurements and records of the conditions that produced them.

**Company evidence:** Data checks are an interpreted work area linked to reliable visualization, not a verified quality job title.
