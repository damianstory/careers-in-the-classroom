## Classroom tab — proposed copy

Storing energy changes when it can be used.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. Electricity demand and available generation can change at different times. A battery can store energy for later use.

2. Power electronics and controls manage electrical transfer between the storage system and other connected equipment.

3. A design must distinguish power, the rate of transfer, from energy, the amount stored or delivered over time.

4. Storage has losses and operating limits. Compare when energy is needed, how quickly it must be delivered and how much is available.

**Discuss:** Could two batteries store the same amount of energy but support different appliances?

**Try thinking it through:** Use fictional devices rated at 1 kW and 3 kW and an idealized 6 kWh store. Compare ideal run times, then ask what changes when losses and power limits are included.

**Teacher note:** Ideal answers are six hours and two hours, only if the device is within the system’s power limit. Real run time depends on usable capacity, operating conditions and losses; these are not Eguana specifications.

**Subject connection:** Physics: energy, power and circuits. Course/outcome mapping remains provisional.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Power-electronics design

Control how electrical energy moves between components. Power-electronics work combines circuit design with testing, thermal limits and the behaviour of the complete system.

Typical tasks:

- Choose and document a circuit design.
- Test its response across operating conditions.
- Investigate losses and component limits before revising the design.

**Works with:** Electrical technologists, firmware developers and manufacturing teams.

**Classroom connection:** Power describes the rate of energy transfer; stored energy describes how much is available.

**Company evidence:** A dated employer posting explicitly establishes Calgary power-electronics engineering work.

### Instrument development and testing

Help build and check the equipment that produces measurements. Instrument work joins hardware, software and experiments rather than treating a reading as automatically correct.

Typical tasks:

- Check performance using a known reference.
- Investigate drift, noise or inconsistent readings.
- Document tests so later users know what the instrument can establish.

**Works with:** Scientists, electronics specialists and software developers.

**Classroom connection:** Measurement uncertainty is part of using a tool responsibly.

**Company evidence:** The posting describes validation and production testing; this work-area label is editorial.

### Software and data systems

Build the tools that move, process and display information. Reliable software needs testing against missing, delayed and unexpected data as well as ideal examples.

Typical tasks:

- Translate a user need into a testable behaviour.
- Build and test a data-processing or display feature.
- Investigate failures and document assumptions about inputs.

**Works with:** Domain experts, designers, users and hardware teams.

**Classroom connection:** An algorithm’s output depends on the quality and meaning of its inputs.

**Company evidence:** Controls and energy-management products support a related software work area; no specific current vacancy is inferred.
