**Can we recover useful water and nutrients from manure?**

**Local note:** Innovation centre and manufacturing plant in Calgary; farm installations are not assumed to be in the city.

## Company tab — proposed copy

Livestock Water Recycling develops manure-treatment systems from a Calgary innovation centre and manufacturing plant. Its PLANT process uses mechanical and chemical treatments to separate solids, nutrient-rich material and water. The company describes uses at livestock operations and other facilities handling nutrient-laden liquids.

**The problem:** How can the components of manure be managed separately rather than as one large mixed stream?

**What it offers:** Treatment systems that separate nutrient streams and recover water for reuse.

**Who uses the work:** Farm operators and other users managing manure or similar nutrient-rich liquids.

**Evidence:** [PLANT process](https://www.livestockwaterrecycling.com/the-system/plant/); [Calgary research work](https://www.livestockwaterrecycling.com/our-story/innovation-center/); [Local facility](https://www.livestockwaterrecycling.com/contact/contact-us/); [Employer role evidence](https://www.livestockwaterrecycling.com/careers/current-job-openings/)

## Classroom tab — proposed copy

Waste contains materials that can be separated and used again.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. Manure is a mixture containing water, solids and nutrients. Treating it as a single stream limits how its components can be managed.

2. LWR describes sequential solids removal and mechanical and chemical treatment in its PLANT system.

3. Separated material can follow different paths: solids, concentrated nutrients and recovered water. Their suitability depends on measurement and intended use.

4. Evaluate the whole system: what happens to every output, how much energy is needed and whether the result meets the requirements for its next use.

**Discuss:** Why is clear-looking water not enough evidence that it is suitable for every use?

**Try thinking it through:** Draw a mass-flow diagram with one incoming stream and three outgoing streams. Ask students to identify measurements needed to account for both water and nutrients.

**Teacher note:** No manure handling is required. The exercise uses a conceptual diagram; concentration and total amount are different quantities.

**Subject connection:** Biology: nutrient cycles; Chemistry: mixtures and concentration; systems thinking. Formal outcomes remain provisional.

**Evidence distinction:** Company process statements come from the sources above. The teaching interpretation, fictional activity and discussion are editorial. No fictional values are company measurements.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Process engineering

Turn a useful transformation into a workable sequence of equipment and operating steps. Process engineers consider what enters, what leaves and what conditions the system must maintain.

Typical tasks:

- Draw a flow of materials and energy through the system.
- Compare operating conditions and equipment options.
- Use trial results to revise the process and communicate constraints.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, operators, mechanical designers and controls specialists.

**Classroom connection:** Conservation of matter and energy connects classroom calculations to process decisions.

**Company evidence:** Treatment-system development is stated; detailed tasks are an editorial explanation.

### Laboratory analysis

Make measurements that other people can trust. A laboratory worker prepares samples, uses a method consistently and records enough detail for someone else to interpret the result.

Typical tasks:

- Prepare and label samples so their identity is preserved.
- Run comparisons or reference samples to check the method.
- Record results, units and limitations before sharing findings.

**Works with:** Scientists, process engineers and quality teams.

**Classroom connection:** A repeatable method and a fair comparison matter as much as a single impressive number.

**Company evidence:** The Calgary centre identifies analytical tools and process research.

### Instrumentation and controls

Keep a process measurable and responsive. Controls work connects sensors, decisions and actuators so the system can respond when conditions change.

Typical tasks:

- Check whether sensors are reading reliably.
- Compare measurements with the intended operating range.
- Test how the system responds to a change or fault.

**Works with:** Operators, electricians and process engineers.

**Classroom connection:** Feedback uses a measured result to inform the next action.

**Company evidence:** Automation is described, and a commissioning/training role names troubleshooting and repair; deployment work can involve US clients.
