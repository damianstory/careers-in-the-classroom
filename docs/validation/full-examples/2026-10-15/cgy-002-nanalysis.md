## Company tab — proposed copy

Nanalysis develops and manufactures compact magnetic-resonance instruments and identifies its head office in Calgary. Its benchtop NMR products are used in areas including teaching, chemistry and food science. This example focuses on chemical analysis rather than medical imaging.

**The problem:** How can an instrument reveal information that cannot be obtained by looking at a sample?

**What it offers:** Benchtop nuclear magnetic resonance instruments and support for their applications.

**Who uses the work:** Laboratories, researchers, educators and industrial users analysing materials.

**Evidence:** [Company, products and Calgary address](https://www.nanalysis.com/); [How the signal becomes a spectrum](https://www.nanalysis.com/nmready-blog/2023/6/27/relation-between-the-fid-and-the-nmr-spectrum); [Careers check](https://www.nanalysis.com/career)

## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A chemical sample can produce a pattern we can interpret.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. Place a suitable sample in the instrument’s magnetic field. NMR concerns the magnetic behaviour of particular atomic nuclei.

2. A radiofrequency pulse changes the sample’s magnetization. The instrument records a time-varying signal.

3. Processing converts the recorded signal into a spectrum. The pattern can provide information about chemical environments.

4. Interpret the spectrum alongside reference information and sample conditions. A simplified pattern is not a photograph of a molecule or an automatic proof of identity.

**Discuss:** What additional evidence would you want if two samples produced similar patterns?

**Try thinking it through:** Show two invented spectra with a shared peak and one different peak. Ask students what they can conclude, what they cannot conclude, and which reference measurement would help.

**Teacher note:** This is a pattern-and-evidence activity, not instruction in assigning real NMR spectra. Avoid describing the signal as X-rays or nuclear radiation.

**Subject connection:** Physics: fields, waves and measurement; Chemistry: chemical identification. Proposed extension, not a verified curriculum outcome.

**Evidence distinction:** Company process statements come from the sources above. The teaching interpretation, fictional activity and discussion are editorial. No fictional values are company measurements.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Instrument development and testing

Help build and check the equipment that produces measurements. Instrument work joins hardware, software and experiments rather than treating a reading as automatically correct.

Typical tasks:

- Check performance using a known reference.
- Investigate drift, noise or inconsistent readings.
- Document tests so later users know what the instrument can establish.

**Works with:** Scientists, electronics specialists and software developers.

**Classroom connection:** Measurement uncertainty is part of using a tool responsibly.

**Company evidence:** The company develops and manufactures instruments; these are related work areas, not verified titles.

### Scientific applications

Connect an instrument to the question a user needs to answer. Applications work involves selecting a method, interpreting results and explaining limitations.

Typical tasks:

- Choose a measurement approach for the sample.
- Compare outputs with reference information.
- Explain what further tests would resolve ambiguity.

**Works with:** Laboratory users, instrument engineers and researchers.

**Classroom connection:** A pattern is evidence to interpret, not a label that proves an answer by itself.

**Company evidence:** Application support is connected to the company’s instrument uses; duties below are editorial.

### Software and data systems

Build the tools that move, process and display information. Reliable software needs testing against missing, delayed and unexpected data as well as ideal examples.

Typical tasks:

- Translate a user need into a testable behaviour.
- Build and test a data-processing or display feature.
- Investigate failures and document assumptions about inputs.

**Works with:** Domain experts, designers, users and hardware teams.

**Classroom connection:** An algorithm’s output depends on the quality and meaning of its inputs.

**Company evidence:** Converting and displaying measurements requires data tools; the role link is interpreted, not a claim of an open position.
