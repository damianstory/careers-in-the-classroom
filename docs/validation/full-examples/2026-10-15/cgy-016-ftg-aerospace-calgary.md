## Classroom tab — proposed copy

An aircraft can carry measurements as well as passengers.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. An aircraft moves through changing atmospheric conditions, so a measurement needs a time, position and altitude to be meaningful.

2. The company describes weather observations from aircraft-mounted systems. The variables available depend on which system is used.

3. Communications and software move observations into tools for users. Reliability includes handling missing or delayed data.

4. Observations along flight routes can add evidence, but they do not sample every place equally. Coverage and instrument differences matter to interpretation.

**Discuss:** How would a map of scheduled flights affect the weather observations available?

**Try thinking it through:** Give students a fictional route map with three aircraft tracks. Ask where observations would be concentrated and which regions would still need another source of measurement.

**Teacher note:** More observations are not the same as uniform coverage. Do not say all aircraft or all company systems measure humidity; the source distinguishes TAMDAR from AMDAR-over-AFIRS.

**Subject connection:** Physics: measurements and signals; atmospheric science: sampling and coverage. Formal course mapping remains provisional.

**Evidence distinction:** Company process statements come from the sources above. The teaching interpretation, fictional activity and discussion are editorial. No fictional values are company measurements.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Avionics and electronics

Work on the electronic systems that allow aircraft to communicate, navigate and collect data. Troubleshooting means distinguishing a faulty sensor from wiring, power or software problems.

Typical tasks:

- Trace a signal through connected components.
- Use test equipment to compare expected and measured behaviour.
- Document the fault and verify the result after authorized work.

**Works with:** Aircraft maintenance teams, electrical designers and software developers.

**Classroom connection:** Circuits and signals become useful when the complete system works reliably.

**Company evidence:** Aircraft hardware and communications are stated product areas.

### Software and data systems

Build the tools that move, process and display information. Reliable software needs testing against missing, delayed and unexpected data as well as ideal examples.

Typical tasks:

- Translate a user need into a testable behaviour.
- Build and test a data-processing or display feature.
- Investigate failures and document assumptions about inputs.

**Works with:** Domain experts, designers, users and hardware teams.

**Classroom connection:** An algorithm’s output depends on the quality and meaning of its inputs.

**Company evidence:** The employer publishes detailed Calgary software roles; one exact card is included.

### Weather-data interpretation

Use observations to understand changing atmospheric conditions. Weather-data work checks where and when a measurement was made before combining it with other evidence.

Typical tasks:

- Check the position, time and quality of observations.
- Compare measurements across altitude or time.
- Identify gaps and explain how they limit an interpretation.

**Works with:** Meteorologists, instrument teams and data engineers.

**Classroom connection:** More measurements can help, but uneven coverage can still leave important gaps.

**Company evidence:** Meteorological interpretation is a related downstream occupation, not a verified Calgary job at the company.
