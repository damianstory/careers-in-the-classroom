## Company tab — proposed copy

AERIUM Analytics is a Calgary-based drone-services company. Its RoBird system is designed to mimic a predatory bird to influence the behaviour of other birds. The company describes applications at airports and other sites, and identifies work with Edmonton International Airport. Calgary is the company connection; the airport example is in the Edmonton region.

**The problem:** Can a controlled wildlife response help reduce conflict around an airport?

**What it offers:** Drone operations, wildlife-management services and spatial-data services.

**Who uses the work:** Airport and site operators managing wildlife interactions and operational risks.

**Evidence:** [RoBird design and airport example](https://www.aeriumanalytics.com/robird/); [Operational tasks](https://www.aeriumanalytics.com/life-at-the-heart-of-aeriums-operations/); [Services and stakeholders](https://www.aeriumanalytics.com/services/); [Calgary connection](https://www.aeriumanalytics.com/contact-us/)

## Classroom tab — proposed copy

An animal’s response can become part of an engineering design.

An illustrative connection between classroom ideas and sourced work.

1. Birds near an airport can create conflicts with aircraft operations. Site teams need to understand where and when birds are present.

2. AERIUM describes RoBird as a falcon-like drone intended to create a deterrence response.

3. Operators coordinate flights with site personnel and observe how birds respond. The drone is one part of a management approach.

4. Compare bird activity and safety outcomes over time while accounting for season, weather and other interventions. A short-term response is not proof of a permanent effect.

**Discuss:** How could you tell whether fewer birds were caused by the intervention or by seasonal migration?

**Try thinking it through:** Create a comparison plan using fictional weekly bird counts at two sites. Ask students which additional variables they would record and why a single before-and-after count is weak evidence.

**Teacher note:** No actual wildlife disturbance or drone flight is part of this activity. Counts are fictional; do not adopt the company’s percentage claims as experimental results.

**Subject connection:** Biology: animal behaviour and environmental interactions; Physics: flight as an extension. Outcome mapping remains provisional.

**Evidence distinction:** Company process statements come from the sources above. The teaching interpretation, fictional activity and discussion are editorial. No fictional values are company measurements.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

The profiles explain kinds of work. The company-evidence note under each profile states how strongly it is linked to this organization. None of these profiles is a promise of a vacancy.

### Drone operations

Plan and carry out flights for a specific task while coordinating with the people responsible for the site. The aircraft is one tool within an operational plan.

Typical tasks:

- Review the task, site and operating constraints.
- Check the aircraft and coordinate the flight with relevant personnel.
- Record observations and equipment issues after the flight.

**Works with:** Site operations, wildlife specialists and data analysts.

**Classroom connection:** Flight performance must be considered alongside the task and the surrounding environment.

**Company evidence:** Company operations material explicitly describes pilots, training and field services.

### Wildlife monitoring and interpretation

Observe how animals respond to an intervention and ask whether the change lasts. This work needs consistent observations and attention to other causes of changing behaviour.

Typical tasks:

- Define how bird activity will be counted.
- Compare observations before and after an intervention.
- Record weather, season and other factors that could explain a difference.

**Works with:** Drone operators, airport staff and environmental teams.

**Classroom connection:** Behavioural response is evidence; reduced sightings alone do not prove a lasting safety outcome.

**Company evidence:** Behavioural monitoring and wildlife management are stated work areas; a specific ecologist job title is not verified.

### Spatial data and mapping

Turn measurements tied to places into usable spatial information. The work involves coordinates, scale and checking that datasets describe the same location and time.

Typical tasks:

- Check coordinate systems and measurement dates.
- Align and inspect spatial datasets.
- Present maps or models with their limitations.

**Works with:** Surveyors, engineers, field operators and software teams.

**Classroom connection:** Models can be detailed while still containing gaps or misaligned measurements.

**Company evidence:** Company services include GIS and mapping work; this is a related work area, not proof every RoBird assignment uses it.
