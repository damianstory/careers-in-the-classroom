## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A scene can be assembled from images recorded at different moments.

1. A camera records light from a scene under a particular set of conditions.

2. Changing the lighting or camera settings can change the appearance of the same subject.

3. An editor arranges selected shots into a sequence; a colourist can work on differences in their appearance.

4. Review the sequence for continuity and meaning. A smooth-looking cut is a creative result, not proof that the events happened continuously.

**Discuss:** How can the order of two shots change what a viewer thinks happened?

**Try thinking it through:** Draw three shots of the same object with different apparent brightness. Propose a continuity check, then reorder the shots to tell a different story and explain the change.

**Teacher note:** This is a visual reasoning exercise. Colour grading changes recorded appearance; it does not change the original light source or prove factual continuity.

### Camera and image capture

Record images that serve a production’s visual and storytelling requirements.

**Typical tasks:**

- Plan framing and camera setup with the production team.
- Check exposure and consistency across takes.
- Record information needed for editing and handoff.

**Works with:** Directors, lighting teams and editors.

**Classroom connection:** The recorded image depends on both the light and how it is captured.

**Company evidence:** Company offers production; specific camera tasks are editorial rather than a verified staff job.

### Film and video editing

Build a sequence from selected footage to communicate a coherent idea.

**Typical tasks:**

- Review and organize available footage.
- Choose cuts, order and timing for the intended story.
- Check continuity and revise against feedback.

**Works with:** Directors, producers, colourists and sound teams.

**Classroom connection:** Sequence can change interpretation even when the individual images stay the same.

**Company evidence:** Company describes editing and post-production services.

### Colour finishing

Work on the appearance and consistency of images during post-production.

**Typical tasks:**

- Compare shots using the agreed visual reference.
- Adjust recorded colour and tonal relationships.
- Check the sequence and deliverable with the production team.

**Works with:** Editors, cinematographers and directors.

**Classroom connection:** Changing the appearance of an image is different from changing the original event.

**Company evidence:** Company about page names colour work; task wording is editorial.
