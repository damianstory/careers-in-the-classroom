## Classroom tab — proposed copy

Waste can reveal a process problem before a new machine is needed.

1. Map the steps from ingredients to saleable product.

2. Measure material losses where they occur instead of combining everything into one waste total.

3. Investigate a cause: a transfer point, a forming step or later handling may need a different response.

4. Test a change and compare both waste and acceptable product output. Moving a loss to another step is not a reduction.

**Discuss:** Why is a smaller waste bin not enough evidence that a process improved?

**Try thinking it through:** For a fictional 100 kg batch, 6 kg is lost at transfer, 4 kg at shaping and 2 kg at packing. A change cuts transfer loss to 3 kg but adds 2 kg at shaping. Calculate the net change and propose one quality check.

**Teacher note:** Fictional totals: loss falls from 12 to 11 kg, a 1 kg improvement. This simplified accounting ignores baking moisture loss and is not a real bakery mass balance.

**Course connection:** Science 10 — energy/material accounting and inquiry; mathematics supports percentages and comparison. Exact science-unit fit remains provisional; the historic assessment is not a chemical-reaction experiment.

### Commercial baking

Produce baked goods through a controlled sequence of preparation, processing and handling.

**Typical tasks:**

- Follow recipes and production schedules.
- Use and check production equipment within trained procedures.
- Observe product consistency and communicate defects or process variation.

**Works with:** Production staff, quality teams and maintenance workers.

**Classroom connection:** Ratios, timing and temperature matter, but quality must still be checked.

**Company evidence:** Employer commercial-baker description explicitly mentions production equipment, recipes and schedules.

### Quality and traceability

Make it possible to explain how a result or product was produced. Quality work checks records, methods and acceptance criteria so variation can be investigated rather than hidden.

**Typical tasks:**

- Check that materials and samples have traceable identities.
- Compare recorded results with defined acceptance criteria.
- Investigate deviations and document what changed.

**Works with:** Production, laboratory, engineering and customer teams.

**Classroom connection:** Repeated results need both measurements and records of the conditions that produced them.

**Company evidence:** Employer emphasizes consistent safe products; the detailed profile is editorial.

### Process engineering

Turn a useful transformation into a workable sequence of equipment and operating steps. Process engineers consider what enters, what leaves and what conditions the system must maintain.

**Typical tasks:**

- Draw a flow of materials and energy through the system.
- Compare operating conditions and equipment options.
- Use trial results to revise the process and communicate constraints.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, operators, mechanical designers and controls specialists.

**Classroom connection:** Conservation of matter and energy connects classroom calculations to process decisions.

**Company evidence:** Published facility assessment supports process-improvement tasks; external partners contributed and are not assumed to be bakery employees.
