## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A sample answers a question about a place, a depth and a moment in time.

1. Define the question and consider how a substance could reach people, water or other organisms.

2. Choose sampling locations and depths that can test that explanation.

3. Compare laboratory results with units, detection limits and the sampling plan visible.

4. Use the evidence to consider treatment, further investigation or monitoring. One clean sample does not describe the whole site.

**Discuss:** How could a sampling plan miss the part of a site that matters most?

**Try thinking it through:** Place six sampling points on a fictional site map containing a former storage area and a drainage ditch. Explain why each point helps test a hypothesis and identify one unsampled uncertainty.

**Teacher note:** This is a reasoning activity, not an environmental site-assessment protocol. Do not supply a universal safe concentration or ask learners to handle unknown soil.

**Course connection:** Chemistry 20 — concentration and sampling; Science 10 — inquiry. Toxicology, site risk and regulatory thresholds are extensions, not simplified safety rules.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

These profiles explain kinds of work, not promises of a job. Company links are qualified separately.

### Environmental field assessment

Collect and interpret evidence about a place before recommending an action.

**Typical tasks:**

- Plan observations or samples around a clear question.
- Record locations, methods and conditions consistently.
- Explain the limits of the evidence and the next useful check.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, engineers and project planners.

**Classroom connection:** A result has meaning only in the context of how and where it was collected.

**Company evidence:** Calgary office and conference author evidence support site investigations.

### Laboratory analysis

Make measurements that other people can trust. A laboratory worker prepares samples, uses a method consistently and records enough detail for someone else to interpret the result.

**Typical tasks:**

- Prepare and label samples so their identity is preserved.
- Run comparisons or reference samples to check the method.
- Record results, units and limitations before sharing findings.

**Works with:** Scientists, process engineers and quality teams.

**Classroom connection:** A repeatable method and a fair comparison matter as much as a single impressive number.

**Company evidence:** Assessment work uses laboratory results; this does not establish that Trace employs an in-house laboratory team.

### Water-resource planning

Compare water availability, demand and possible responses over time.

**Typical tasks:**

- Organize supply, use and monitoring information.
- Test alternative assumptions in scenarios.
- Explain trade-offs, uncertainty and priorities.

**Works with:** Hydrologists, engineers, policy specialists and communities.

**Classroom connection:** A model is a way to test assumptions, not a guarantee about the future.

**Company evidence:** Company water and risk services support the connection; this is not a specific job posting.
