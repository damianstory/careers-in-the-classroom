## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A treatment idea has to keep working when real water and operating conditions change.

1. Identify what is in the incoming water and what quality is required for the intended discharge or reuse.

2. Select treatment steps that address the target substances. A biological reactor is one possible component, not a universal solution.

3. Equipment, controls and maintenance must keep the process within suitable conditions.

4. Measure the outgoing water and compare it with the requirement. A promising test is not the same as sustained operating performance.

**Discuss:** What could change between a successful small trial and a full operating plant?

**Try thinking it through:** Compare two fictional plants with equal average output quality: one has stable results and one has large swings. Decide what additional operating records would help explain the difference.

**Teacher note:** Do not infer compliance from an average or equate all biological treatment processes. No claimed removal percentage is used.

**Course connection:** Chemistry 20 — aqueous mixtures and concentration; Biology links to microbial processes are supporting extensions. Detailed reactor design is beyond the school outcome.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

These profiles explain kinds of work, not promises of a job. Company links are qualified separately.

### Process engineering

Turn a useful transformation into a workable sequence of equipment and operating steps. Process engineers consider what enters, what leaves and what conditions the system must maintain.

**Typical tasks:**

- Draw a flow of materials and energy through the system.
- Compare operating conditions and equipment options.
- Use trial results to revise the process and communicate constraints.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, operators, mechanical designers and controls specialists.

**Classroom connection:** Conservation of matter and energy connects classroom calculations to process decisions.

**Company evidence:** Company treatment-design services support process engineering.

### Water-treatment operations

Keep a treatment process working within its specified conditions and record how it performs.

**Typical tasks:**

- Monitor process readings and output tests.
- Follow operating and maintenance procedures.
- Document deviations and coordinate investigation.

**Works with:** Process engineers, laboratories and maintenance teams.

**Classroom connection:** Stable operation matters even when average results look acceptable.

**Company evidence:** Company project material describes treatment operations; not necessarily a Calgary plant or vacancy.

### Instrumentation and controls

Keep a process measurable and responsive. Controls work connects sensors, decisions and actuators so the system can respond when conditions change.

**Typical tasks:**

- Check whether sensors are reading reliably.
- Compare measurements with the intended operating range.
- Test how the system responds to a change or fault.

**Works with:** Operators, electricians and process engineers.

**Classroom connection:** Feedback uses a measured result to inform the next action.

**Company evidence:** Integrated systems require controls; this is an interpreted work connection, not a named employer posting.
