## Classroom tab — proposed copy

The layers around a room have several jobs, and they must work together.

1. Indoor and outdoor conditions differ, so heat and moisture can move across a building enclosure.

2. Materials and joints perform different jobs: insulation, air control, water control and structural support are not interchangeable.

3. A defect at a joint can matter even when the main wall materials perform well.

4. Inspection and measurements help identify a cause before a repair is chosen. Appearance alone may not explain the problem.

**Discuss:** Why might adding more insulation fail to fix a water leak?

**Try thinking it through:** Annotate a fictional wall section with separate arrows for heat flow, air leakage and rainwater. Identify one joint to inspect and one measurement that would distinguish competing explanations.

**Teacher note:** The section is conceptual, not construction advice. Correct assemblies depend on climate, materials, codes and professional design.

**Course connection:** Science 10 — energy flow in technological systems. Moisture transport and enclosure detailing are extensions; avoid claiming a specific vapour-barrier placement is universally correct.

- Support construction records and field verification.

**Works with:** Engineers, surveyors and site teams.

### Quality and traceability

Make it possible to explain how a result or product was produced. Quality work checks records, methods and acceptance criteria so variation can be investigated rather than hidden.

**Typical tasks:**

- Check that materials and samples have traceable identities.
- Compare recorded results with defined acceptance criteria.
- Investigate deviations and document what changed.

**Works with:** Production, laboratory, engineering and customer teams.

**Classroom connection:** Repeated results need both measurements and records of the conditions that produced them.
