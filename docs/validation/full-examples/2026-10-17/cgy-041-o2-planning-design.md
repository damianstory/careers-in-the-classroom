## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A park can be part of a city’s water system.

1. Runoff carries water and suspended material from streets into drainage systems.

2. At Dale Hodges Park, water follows a designed route through a landscape rather than remaining hidden in pipes.

3. Slower flow can allow some suspended particles to settle. Different treatment stages address different parts of the problem.

4. The landscape also supports habitat and recreation. A useful design must account for these needs together.

**Discuss:** Would the clearest-looking water necessarily be the safest water?

**Try thinking it through:** Draw two fictional routes from a street drain to a river: one short and fast, one with space for settling. Mark where to sample and one habitat trade-off.

**Teacher note:** Clear water can still contain dissolved contaminants. This is a planning model, not a drinking-water treatment demonstration.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

These profiles explain kinds of work, not promises of a job. Company links are qualified separately.

### Landscape design

Shape outdoor spaces so water, habitat and human uses can work together.

**Typical tasks:**

- Read the site and identify competing needs.
- Develop plans and sections that show landforms and planting.
- Review details with engineers, ecologists and construction teams.

**Works with:** Ecologists, planners, engineers and contractors.

**Classroom connection:** A change to a landform changes paths for both water and people.

**Company evidence:** O2 lists landscape architects and a Calgary junior-designer description; this role summary is editorial.

### Spatial data and mapping

Turn measurements tied to places into usable spatial information. The work involves coordinates, scale and checking that datasets describe the same location and time.

**Typical tasks:**

- Check coordinate systems and measurement dates.
- Align and inspect spatial datasets.
- Present maps or models with their limitations.

**Works with:** Surveyors, engineers, field operators and software teams.

**Classroom connection:** Models can be detailed while still containing gaps or misaligned measurements.

**Company evidence:** O2’s mapping page describes GIS and visualization services.

### Environmental field assessment

Collect and interpret evidence about a place before recommending an action.

**Typical tasks:**

- Plan observations or samples around a clear question.
- Record locations, methods and conditions consistently.
- Explain the limits of the evidence and the next useful check.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, engineers and project planners.

**Classroom connection:** A result has meaning only in the context of how and where it was collected.

**Company evidence:** O2 lists environmental scientists; the park project supports ecological work, not a particular employee assignment.
