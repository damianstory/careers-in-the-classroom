## Classroom tab — proposed copy

The space below a building can be just as important as the rooms inside it.

1. A building’s weight and the people inside create loads that must reach the ground.

2. An active rail corridor limits where supports can go. The structure has to span the space.

3. Truss members and connections transfer forces along a designed load path. Construction stages need their own checks.

4. Engineers coordinate the drawings and sequence with builders and transit staff so the permanent design can be built.

**Discuss:** Why might a structure that works when complete need extra support during construction?

**Try thinking it through:** Sketch a fictional floor across a no-support zone. Trace a load path to the ground and identify a connection that would need checking.

**Teacher note:** Conceptual force arrows are not an engineered design. Do not suggest students can assess a real building’s safety from a drawing.

**Course connection:** Physics 20 — forces and dynamics. Load paths are an application; truss design and detailed structural calculations are extensions.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

These profiles explain kinds of work, not promises of a job. Company links are qualified separately.

### Structural engineering

Work out how a structure transfers loads through members and connections to its supports.

**Typical tasks:**

- Identify loads and constraints in the design brief.
- Develop and check a structural model and details.
- Coordinate construction-stage requirements with the project team.

**Works with:** Architects, technologists, builders and reviewers.

**Classroom connection:** Forces must have a continuous route to supports; an attractive shape alone is not evidence of performance.

**Company evidence:** Entuitive identifies its structural engineering contribution and Calgary project lead.

### Civil and construction technology

Turn project requirements into usable drawings, quantities and checks.

**Typical tasks:**

- Prepare technical drawings using measured site information.
- Check dimensions and interfaces between design disciplines.
- Support construction records and field verification.

**Works with:** Engineers, surveyors and site teams.

**Classroom connection:** Scale and accurate measurements connect a drawing to the real location.

**Company evidence:** Technical detailing is documented in the project account; this is not a verified vacancy.

### Construction coordination

Help different teams work from compatible information and an achievable sequence.

**Typical tasks:**

- Track design changes and unresolved interfaces.
- Compare construction stages with access and operating constraints.
- Communicate decisions and confirm that the correct documents reach the field.

**Works with:** Designers, contractors, owners and operations staff.

**Classroom connection:** A system that works at the end may need different support while it is being assembled.

**Company evidence:** The project account describes construction sequencing and transit coordination; role label is editorial.
