## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A railway moves through space, but its plan also has to work through time.

1. Two trains can need the same section of track at different times. Distance alone does not reveal a conflict.

2. A time-distance diagram lets a planner compare movement and waiting in one view.

3. Changing one train’s departure may affect several later movements. Plans have to consider constraints together.

4. Real rail operations use formal rules, communication and trained staff. A classroom timetable illustrates only one part of that system.

**Discuss:** What happens to the rest of a timetable when one train is delayed?

**Try thinking it through:** On a fictional single-track segment, Train A occupies it from minute 0 to 8 and Train B from minute 5 to 12. Shift B to remove overlap, then discuss what additional safety margin would need a real rule rather than a guess.

**Teacher note:** This is a scheduling puzzle, not a safe operating procedure. Do not invent braking distances or dispatch clearances.

**Course connection:** Physics 20 — position-time graphs and motion; scheduling and network optimization are applications, not claimed required outcomes.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

These profiles explain kinds of work, not promises of a job. Company links are qualified separately.

### Rail traffic control

Coordinate railway movements and crews within formal operating systems and rules.

**Typical tasks:**

- Monitor movement information and operating constraints.
- Coordinate plans and communicate with railway teams.
- Respond to changes using the applicable procedures and authority.

**Works with:** Train crews, operations managers and maintenance teams.

**Classroom connection:** Time and position together reveal conflicts that a map alone can miss.

**Company evidence:** 2025 company annual report explicitly identifies controllers and Calgary operations.

### Railway conductor work

Support train movements and handling of rail vehicles through trained railway operations.

**Typical tasks:**

- Apply operating procedures during assigned movements.
- Communicate with other railway workers.
- Check and document required operational information.

**Works with:** Locomotive crews, controllers and yard teams.

**Classroom connection:** The plan and the physical movement must agree; conductor and dispatcher are different roles.

**Company evidence:** SAIT describes conductor tasks as related railway work; no specific Calgary CPKC vacancy or project assignment is claimed.

### Software and data systems

Build the tools that move, process and display information. Reliable software needs testing against missing, delayed and unexpected data as well as ideal examples.

**Typical tasks:**

- Translate a user need into a testable behaviour.
- Build and test a data-processing or display feature.
- Investigate failures and document assumptions about inputs.

**Works with:** Domain experts, designers, users and hardware teams.

**Classroom connection:** An algorithm’s output depends on the quality and meaning of its inputs.

**Company evidence:** Adjacent network/data work; not a verified CPKC job or requirement for traffic control.
