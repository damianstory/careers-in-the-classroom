## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A useful animation helps an audience follow relationships that a camera cannot easily show.

1. Start with the audience and one question the explanation must answer.

2. Turn the idea into a script and storyboard that show the sequence before polishing the artwork.

3. Use timing, viewpoint and visual choices to make a relationship visible. A model may simplify reality, so the simplification should be deliberate.

4. Review whether a viewer understood the intended idea. Revise a confusing scene instead of adding decoration.

**Discuss:** When does simplifying an animation make it misleading?

**Try thinking it through:** Draw four frames showing a ball at equal time intervals. Make one version constant-speed and one speeding up. Ask a partner to infer the motion from the spacing.

**Teacher note:** The ball exercise is our physics extension, not a claimed Broken Pencil client project. Artistic motion may intentionally depart from realistic motion.

### Storyboarding and illustration

Decide what an audience needs to see before the final sequence is produced.

**Typical tasks:**

- Translate a message into visual scenes.
- Check order, viewpoint and transitions using rough drawings.
- Revise the sequence after feedback before detailed animation.

**Works with:** Writers, animators and clients.

**Classroom connection:** A model needs to preserve the relationship it is intended to explain.

**Company evidence:** Company production article describes script-to-storyboard and illustration work.

### Animation

Create motion from planned images, models and timing decisions.

**Typical tasks:**

- Set key positions and the time between them.
- Adjust spacing and transitions to communicate movement.
- Review the result against the story and technical requirements.

**Works with:** Illustrators, directors and sound specialists.

**Classroom connection:** Spacing at equal time intervals can communicate constant speed or changing speed.

**Company evidence:** Company article explicitly describes animation and timing.

### Creative production coordination

Help a project move from a communication brief to a deliverable that can be reviewed.

**Typical tasks:**

- Clarify audience, scope and review milestones.
- Coordinate feedback between creative and client teams.
- Track revisions and confirm delivery requirements.

**Works with:** Writers, designers, animators and clients.

**Classroom connection:** Defining the question first helps distinguish useful evidence from extra detail.
