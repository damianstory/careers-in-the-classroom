## Classroom tab — proposed copy

A route has to work for people and for the ground and water beneath it.

1. Map the terrain, existing connections and watercourse before drawing a route.

2. Compare options for gradients, crossings and access. A change in one constraint can affect another.

3. Bring transportation, structural, water and environmental expertise together to develop the design.

4. Check the built corridor against its intended function and monitor the landscape it affects. Design intent is not a measured ecological outcome.

**Discuss:** Why might the shortest route be a poor design?

**Try thinking it through:** Choose between two fictional corridors: one is shorter but steep and crosses a creek twice; one is longer and crosses once. List evidence needed before selecting either, without inventing a numerical score.

**Teacher note:** The exercise is not the actual route evaluation. No claim that the real creek project had a measured biodiversity improvement is made.

**Course connection:** Physics 20 — gradients and motion as an extension; Science 10 — systems/inquiry. A bridge can support force discussions, but detailed transportation design is not a school outcome.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

These profiles explain kinds of work, not promises of a job. Company links are qualified separately.

### Civil and construction technology

Turn project requirements into usable drawings, quantities and checks.

**Typical tasks:**

- Prepare technical drawings using measured site information.
- Check dimensions and interfaces between design disciplines.
- Support construction records and field verification.

**Works with:** Engineers, surveyors and site teams.

**Classroom connection:** Scale and accurate measurements connect a drawing to the real location.

**Company evidence:** Project account describes planning/design/construction and engineering disciplines.

### Spatial data and mapping

Turn measurements tied to places into usable spatial information. The work involves coordinates, scale and checking that datasets describe the same location and time.

**Typical tasks:**

- Check coordinate systems and measurement dates.
- Align and inspect spatial datasets.
- Present maps or models with their limitations.

**Works with:** Surveyors, engineers, field operators and software teams.

**Classroom connection:** Models can be detailed while still containing gaps or misaligned measurements.

**Company evidence:** Site and route work support mapping; no specific surveyor posting is claimed.

### Environmental field assessment

Collect and interpret evidence about a place before recommending an action.

**Typical tasks:**

- Plan observations or samples around a clear question.
- Record locations, methods and conditions consistently.
- Explain the limits of the evidence and the next useful check.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, engineers and project planners.

**Classroom connection:** A result has meaning only in the context of how and where it was collected.

**Company evidence:** Project account identifies environmental management and creek work.
