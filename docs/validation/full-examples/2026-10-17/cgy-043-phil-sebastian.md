## Classroom tab — proposed copy

Consistency depends on checking what happened, not just repeating a recipe.

1. A roast changes a batch of green coffee through heating. Time and temperature matter, but they do not describe every property of the result.

2. The company says it records roast data and checks samples with a reflectometer.

3. A reflectance reading measures how light returns from the sample under specified conditions; it is not a direct measurement of flavour.

4. Comparing process records with product measurements can reveal variation and guide the next controlled test.

**Discuss:** If two batches have the same recorded temperature history, what else would you want to measure?

**Try thinking it through:** Compare three fictional batches: A and B share a heating record but have different colour readings; B and C share colour readings but have different sensory notes. List what each comparison can and cannot show.

**Teacher note:** The fictional comparisons are not company results. No coffee consumption or hot-equipment activity is required.

**Course connection:** Science 10 — energy flow and measurement; Physics 30 — light and reflectance as an extension. A roast-colour reading does not establish a spectrum or chemical composition.

## Roles tab — proposed copy

These profiles explain kinds of work, not promises of a job. Company links are qualified separately.

### Roast production

Operate a controlled food-processing sequence and compare the product with its intended result.

**Typical tasks:**

- Track batch identity and process settings.
- Record observations and measurements during production.
- Report variation and help compare trial adjustments.

**Works with:** Quality staff, technicians and production planners.

**Classroom connection:** Repeating a setting is not the same as verifying a repeatable result.

**Company evidence:** Company process description supports roasting work; no specific roaster vacancy verified.

### Instrument development and testing

Help build and check the equipment that produces measurements. Instrument work joins hardware, software and experiments rather than treating a reading as automatically correct.

**Typical tasks:**

- Check performance using a known reference.
- Investigate drift, noise or inconsistent readings.
- Document tests so later users know what the instrument can establish.

**Works with:** Scientists, electronics specialists and software developers.

**Classroom connection:** Measurement uncertainty is part of using a tool responsibly.

**Company evidence:** Custom equipment and reflectance measurements support the work-area connection.

### Quality and traceability

Make it possible to explain how a result or product was produced. Quality work checks records, methods and acceptance criteria so variation can be investigated rather than hidden.

**Typical tasks:**

- Check that materials and samples have traceable identities.
- Compare recorded results with defined acceptance criteria.
- Investigate deviations and document what changed.

**Works with:** Production, laboratory, engineering and customer teams.

**Classroom connection:** Repeated results need both measurements and records of the conditions that produced them.

**Company evidence:** Company describes batch checks; the general quality profile is not a job description.
