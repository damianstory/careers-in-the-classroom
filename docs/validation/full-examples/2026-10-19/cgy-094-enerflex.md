## How it works

1. Define the required gas-flow and pressure change for a system.
2. A compressor does work on the gas; the temperature may also change, depending on the process and heat transfer.
3. Designers coordinate the compressor with cooling, controls and other equipment.
4. Assembly and service teams check the physical system and maintain it through its working life.

These steps are a sourced classroom explanation. They are not an employer operating procedure, an exact facility diagram or proof of measured outcomes.

## Classroom conversation

Why is a pressure target not enough information to design a compression system?

**Activity:** For an explicitly constant-temperature model, compare a gas at 1 volume unit and 100 pressure units with half that volume. Predict the pressure ratio, then explain why a real rapidly operating compressor may depart from the model.

**Teacher note:** The idealised isothermal result is not a specification or description of a real Enerflex compressor. Compression work, temperature change and heat removal must not be conflated.

**Proposed role ID:** work-mechanical · existing shared profile

Translate a need into a component or machine that can be built and tested. Designers use dimensions, material properties and constraints to evaluate alternatives.

### Industrial machinery assembly and maintenance

**Proposed role ID:** work-millwright · existing shared profile

Make machinery work together as a physical system. Millwright work connects assembly, alignment, inspection and troubleshooting.



- **Investigate:** Read equipment drawings and plan assembly steps.
- **Test and compare:** Align and connect mechanical components.
- **Explain and improve:** Check operation and record problems that need adjustment.

**Works with:** Designers, operators, electricians and fabrication teams.

**Classroom connection:** Small alignment errors can change how forces and motion pass through a machine.
