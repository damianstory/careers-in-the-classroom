## How it works

1. Incoming scrap must be identified and assessed for suitability and contamination.
2. Different metal categories are kept apart because composition affects their later use.
3. Receiving and handling procedures protect the quality and safety of the material stream.
4. Separated material moves onward for further recovery; sorting itself does not turn every item into new metal.

These steps are a sourced classroom explanation. They are not an employer operating procedure, an exact facility diagram or proof of measured outcomes.

## Classroom conversation

Why is a magnet useful but insufficient for identifying every scrap-metal item?

**Activity:** Give groups fictional scrap cards with known labels, magnetic response and contamination notes. Ask them to design categories and flag items needing more information. Discuss why unknown chemistry cannot be solved by appearance alone.

**Teacher note:** Paper-only material cards; do not handle unknown scrap or test hazardous containers. Iron-containing alloys do not all have identical magnetic behaviour, and sorting is not the same as chemical refining.
