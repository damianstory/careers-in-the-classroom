## How it works

1. Milk begins as a mixture containing water, proteins, fat and other components.
2. In a general cheesemaking model, acid and/or enzymes help form a protein network.
3. Separating curds from whey changes how much liquid remains with the curd.
4. Moisture, treatment and other process choices affect the resulting texture; different cheeses follow different methods.

These steps are a sourced classroom explanation. They are not an employer operating procedure, an exact facility diagram or proof of measured outcomes.

## Classroom conversation

Why can the same starting material lead to different textures?

**Activity:** Use paper counters to represent water, fat and protein in a fictional batch. Model a curd/whey split and explain what this model shows—and what it cannot explain about flavour, safety or texture.

**Teacher note:** Paper model only. Coagulation is not simply filtering all solids from milk. Do not offer a food-production recipe or assign a generic method to all Franco’s products.
