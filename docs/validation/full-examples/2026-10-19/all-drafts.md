## How it works

1. A practitioner discusses the person’s activities and measures the limb and movement needs.
2. Measurements and a cast/model help define a device that must fit this individual.
3. A trial fitting lets the team examine comfort, alignment and movement together.
4. The team revises the device and follows up; a good-looking object is not automatically a useful fit.

These steps are a sourced classroom explanation. They are not an employer operating procedure, an exact facility diagram or proof of measured outcomes.

## Classroom conversation

Why could a device that fits its measurements still need adjustment?

**Activity:** On paper, draw the support forces on a simplified artificial foot while a person is standing still. Explain which observations—fit, movement or comfort—the force diagram cannot supply.

**Teacher note:** The force diagram is a classroom model, not an analysis of a real patient or advice on fitting a prosthesis. Do not imply that technicians independently make clinical prescriptions.
