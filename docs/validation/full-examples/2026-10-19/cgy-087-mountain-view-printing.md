## How it works

1. A design is prepared for a particular stock and printing process.
2. A common four-colour process places cyan, magenta, yellow and black patterns on the sheet.
3. Ink absorbs some incoming light; the light reaching the viewer is reflected from the printed surface.
4. A production worker checks colour, alignment and image quality and corrects permitted settings.

These steps are a sourced classroom explanation. They are not an employer operating procedure, an exact facility diagram or proof of measured outcomes.

## Classroom conversation

Why might a colour look different on a screen and on a printed sheet?

**Activity:** Compare an enlarged printed colour image with a screen image using photographs or a classroom magnifier. Sketch dot patterns and identify what illumination, paper and alignment could change. No machine operation is involved.

**Teacher note:** CMYK describes subtractive print colour, not red/green/blue emitted-light mixing. The four-colour model is an application extension, and some printing uses different inks or methods.
