## How it works

1. Identify what a particular valve is intended to do and under what conditions.
2. Inspect or diagnose its condition before deciding what service is needed.
3. Use the appropriate controlled test to compare observed behaviour with the required behaviour.
4. Document results and any further action; one passing observation does not establish every aspect of future reliability.

These steps are a sourced classroom explanation. They are not an employer operating procedure, an exact facility diagram or proof of measured outcomes.

## Classroom conversation

Why is seeing a valve open once not a complete test?

**Activity:** Use a drawn valve with a fictional spring force of 100 N opposing a pressure force of 80 N, then 120 N. Draw net-force directions and explain why this simplified force comparison is not a real pressure-valve setting.

**Teacher note:** Physics force model only. No pressure equipment is assembled or tested in class. Control valves regulate flow; relief devices protect against overpressure and should not be treated as interchangeable.

**Proposed role ID:** work-valve-service · new profile

Inspect, repair and test valves used to control or relieve process flow.

- **Service:** Carry out permitted repairs and reassembly.
- **Test:** Use approved test equipment and document the result.

**Works with:** Instrumentation staff, Operations, Quality and safety teams.

### Instrumentation and controls

**Proposed role ID:** work-controls · existing shared profile

Keep a process measurable and responsive. Controls work connects sensors, decisions and actuators so the system can respond when conditions change.



- **Investigate:** Check whether sensors are reading reliably.
- **Test and compare:** Compare measurements with the intended operating range.
- **Explain and improve:** Test how the system responds to a change or fault.

**Works with:** Operators, electricians and process engineers.
