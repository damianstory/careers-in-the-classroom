## How it works

1. Decide what quantity the process needs and the conditions in which it must be measured.
2. Compare the instrument’s output with an appropriate known reference or proving method.
3. Examine repeatability and differences; several similar readings can still be biased.
4. Investigate or adjust the system as permitted, then verify again and keep the result documented.

These steps are a sourced classroom explanation. They are not an employer operating procedure, an exact facility diagram or proof of measured outcomes.

## Classroom conversation

Could a meter give the same reading every time and still be wrong?

**Activity:** A fictional reference delivers 200 mL. Meter A reports 210, 210 and 211 mL; meter B reports 198, 202 and 200 mL. Compare repeatability with agreement to the reference. State a limitation of only three trials.

**Teacher note:** Invented data, not a Westech test result. Calibration, adjustment and proof of fitness for a particular industrial use are different things.

### Instrumentation and controls

**Proposed role ID:** work-controls · existing shared profile

Keep a process measurable and responsive. Controls work connects sensors, decisions and actuators so the system can respond when conditions change.

- **Test and compare:** Compare measurements with the intended operating range.
