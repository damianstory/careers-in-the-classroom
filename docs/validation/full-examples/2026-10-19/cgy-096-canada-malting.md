## How it works

1. Suitable grain is selected and checked before entering production.
2. Steeping increases moisture and starts the conditions for germination.
3. Controlled germination allows biological changes, including development of enzyme systems.
4. Kilning dries the grain and shapes product characteristics, bringing this growth stage under control.

These steps are a sourced classroom explanation. They are not an employer operating procedure, an exact facility diagram or proof of measured outcomes.

## Classroom conversation

Why would stopping growth at the right stage be as important as starting it?

**Activity:** Compare invented daily records for grain moisture, growth stage and temperature. Decide what further observations would help distinguish an intended process change from uncontrolled spoilage. Do not grow mould or produce food in this activity.

**Teacher note:** Malting is controlled grain germination and drying; it is not itself fermentation. Enzyme and seed examples extend core biology; do not call a growth record a company operating recipe.

### Process and plant operations

**Proposed role ID:** process-operations · existing shared profile

Keep processing equipment running safely and learn from its measurements.

Operators put a process into practice. They inspect equipment, record operating conditions and work with engineers and maintenance teams when results differ from what was expected.

- **Keep useful records:** Document conditions and events so results can be compared across shifts and tests.
- **Troubleshoot safely:** Report problems, support maintenance and help with commissioning or start-up work.

### Laboratory analysis

**Proposed role ID:** work-lab · existing shared profile

Make measurements that other people can trust. A laboratory worker prepares samples, uses a method consistently and records enough detail for someone else to interpret the result.



- **Investigate:** Prepare and label samples so their identity is preserved.
- **Test and compare:** Run comparisons or reference samples to check the method.
- **Explain and improve:** Record results, units and limitations before sharing findings.

**Works with:** Scientists, process engineers and quality teams.

**Classroom connection:** A repeatable method and a fair comparison matter as much as a single impressive number.
