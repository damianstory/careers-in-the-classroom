## How it works

1. A client and designer clarify the purpose, dimensions and design constraints.
2. Sketches or CAD make the proposal visible and discussable.
3. A render or optional wax model helps reveal changes to shape and proportions before final production.
4. Review, production coordination and later fitting connect the model to the final object.

These steps are a sourced classroom explanation. They are not an employer operating procedure, an exact facility diagram or proof of measured outcomes.

## Classroom conversation

What can a wax model tell you that a polished digital render might hide?

**Activity:** Design a paper model for a fictional ring and compare its drawn dimensions with the folded model. List which questions concern geometry, material behaviour, appearance or manufacturing—and which the paper model cannot answer.

**Teacher note:** Cross-curricular design inquiry, not a claim that jewellery is a required physics unit. No metal melting, casting or clinical sizing activity.
