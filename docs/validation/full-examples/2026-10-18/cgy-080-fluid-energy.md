## How it works

1. Start with the task: which deposit or material needs to be affected, and which surrounding materials should be preserved?
2. Specify the solution and test conditions. Acid strength, concentration and a formulation's overall behaviour are different ideas.
3. Compare performance under the same conditions and record more than one outcome, such as the target effect and unwanted material change.
4. Choose a formulation against the whole task. A single pH reading or fast reaction does not describe every handling, corrosion or environmental property.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

## Classroom conversation

Could two solutions with the same pH still require different evidence before choosing between them?

**Activity:** Use paper-only fictional data for formulations A and B: both meet a target-removal criterion, but one changes an equipment-material sample more. Ask students to identify controlled variables, the missing repeat measurements and a second decision criterion. Do not provide a recipe.

**Teacher note:** No classroom handling of the company's products is proposed. 'Modified' does not mean harmless. Keep strong/weak acid, concentrated/dilute solution and overall hazard distinct.

### Materials and formulation research

Investigate how a material's composition and structure relate to what it does.

- Define a property that matters for the intended use.
- Compare candidate materials using a stated method.
- Explain trade-offs and what evidence is still missing.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, Manufacturing teams, Application specialists.

**Classroom connection:** A promising material must be evaluated against a specific task.

**Related routes in this draft:** SAIT — Chemical Laboratory Technology; University of Calgary — Chemistry or Biochemistry. These are connections to explore, not employer hiring requirements.

### Laboratory testing and analysis

Produce measurements that are useful because samples and comparisons are controlled.

- Label and prepare samples so their identity stays traceable.
- Change one planned factor while holding comparison conditions steady.
- Report results with units, repeats and limitations.

**Works with:** Scientists, Quality teams, Process engineers.

**Classroom connection:** Fair comparisons are more informative than an isolated result.

**Related routes in this draft:** SAIT — Chemical Laboratory Technology; University of Calgary — Chemistry or Biochemistry. These are connections to explore, not employer hiring requirements.

### Process and systems engineering

Connect material and energy changes to a sequence of equipment and operating choices.

- Draw what enters and leaves each part of a process.
- Compare equipment choices against the required conditions.
- Use test results to revise the design and explain its limits.

**Works with:** Operators, Laboratory staff, Mechanical designers.

**Classroom connection:** Conservation of matter and energy helps reveal missing assumptions.

**Related routes in this draft:** SAIT — Chemical Laboratory Technology; SAIT — Instrumentation Engineering Technology. These are connections to explore, not employer hiring requirements.
