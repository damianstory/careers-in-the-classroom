## How it works

1. Concrete combines a binder, water, aggregates and sometimes admixtures. Cement is one ingredient, not another name for the whole mixture.
2. Before hardening, the mixture must be workable enough for its placement method.
3. Cement hydration is a chemical process. Hardening is not simply the water evaporating and leaving dry powder behind.
4. The finished material must meet the job's requirements. Mix selection, delivery and testing address different parts of that requirement.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

## Classroom conversation

Why might a mixture that is easier to place still need separate evidence about its hardened performance?

**Activity:** Compare three invented mix cards using only ordinal scores: workability, early strength evidence and transport-time tolerance. Give groups different project needs and ask them to choose a mix and identify a missing test. The cards are decision models, not real recipes or engineering specifications.

**Teacher note:** Do not imply that adding water is a universal improvement. No student mixing of industrial concrete is required. Exact hydration chemistry and structural mix design remain outside this lesson.
