Alberta Boot Company handcrafts boots at a Calgary workshop connected to its flagship store. ## How it works

1. A maker selects materials and plans flat pieces that must work together.
2. Cutting and joining those pieces requires attention to how the material stretches and how parts align.
3. Lasting shapes the assembled upper around a form. A flat pattern must become a three-dimensional shape.
4. The finished boot has to balance fit, flexibility, appearance and contact with the ground. No single property is best for every intended use.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

## Classroom conversation

Which property would you refuse to sacrifice for your chosen use, and what trade-off would that create?

**Activity:** Give students two fictional outsole samples on the same horizontal surface with the same normal load. Compare the horizontal force just before sliding, then discuss why this test says little about comfort, fit or long-term wear. Treat this as a model, not Alberta Boot's testing procedure.

**Teacher note:** For the friction model compare the same surface and load; do not label a material universally 'grippier'. The actual company evidence supports craft and material decisions, not measured outsole performance.
