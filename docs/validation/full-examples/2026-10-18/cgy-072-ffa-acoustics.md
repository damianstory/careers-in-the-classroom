## How it works

1. A source sends sound toward a listener and the room's surfaces.
2. Direct sound reaches the listener along one path; reflected sound arrives along others.
3. Surface choices and room geometry affect how sound is reflected, absorbed and spread. Repeated reflections can make speech harder to distinguish.
4. Consultants measure and model the space, then compare changes with the room's purpose. A concert space and a meeting room need not share the same goal.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

## Classroom conversation

Would removing every reflection necessarily make a room better for every purpose?

**Activity:** Draw two imaginary rooms with identical dimensions and one sound source. Give one room more sound-absorbing surfaces. Trace direct and reflected paths and predict a difference. Identify what would need measurement before calling one room better.

**Teacher note:** Absorption and sound isolation are different: reducing reflections inside a room is not the same as stopping sound reaching the next room. Reverberation and perceived clarity are not interchangeable with volume.

## Roles

### Acoustical consulting

Investigate how sound behaves in a space and recommend changes for its intended use.

- Measure or model sound paths and levels.
- Compare changes to room surfaces, layout or noise isolation.
- Explain the trade-offs to the people designing or using the space.

**Works with:** Architects, Mechanical engineers, Building users.

**Classroom connection:** Reflection and absorption become decisions about how a room works.

**Related routes in this draft:** University of Calgary — Mechanical Engineering; SAIT — Mechanical Engineering Technology. These are connections to explore, not employer hiring requirements.

### Instrumentation and control

Help a system turn physical conditions into dependable readings and actions.

- Choose a measurement point and check the instrument's range.
- Compare readings with a reference and investigate missing or unexpected values.
- Use evidence to adjust a control or recommend a closer inspection.

**Works with:** Operators, Engineers, Maintenance teams.

**Classroom connection:** A reading depends on where and how it was measured, not just the number displayed.

**Related routes in this draft:** SAIT — Mechanical Engineering Technology. These are connections to explore, not employer hiring requirements.
