## How it works

1. A remote device needs a dependable supply of electrical energy.
2. In the company's described thermoelectric system, a burner supplies heat to a hot side while cooling maintains a lower temperature on the other side.
3. That temperature difference supports a voltage across thermoelectric elements; an attached circuit can supply DC power to a load.
4. Only part of the supplied energy becomes useful electrical output. The rest must still be accounted for, including heat released to the surroundings.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

## Classroom conversation

Why would cooling matter in a machine whose purpose is to use heat?

**Activity:** Draw an illustrative energy account with 100 units supplied, 6 units delivered electrically and 94 units transferred elsewhere. Calculate the model efficiency, then explain why these invented values cannot be presented as a product rating. Mark the hot and cool sides and the separate electrical circuit.

**Teacher note:** The Seebeck effect is an extension. Keep the core lesson on energy conversion and constraints. These systems use an energy source; 'no moving parts' does not mean free energy, no fuel or no emissions.
