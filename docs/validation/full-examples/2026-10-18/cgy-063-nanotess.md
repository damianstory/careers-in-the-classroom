## How it works

1. Start with a testable question: does adding a candidate material change the rate of a defined reaction?
2. Compare otherwise similar systems with and without that material, using a method that measures change over time.
3. A catalyst can provide an alternative reaction pathway. It does not supply unlimited energy or change the reaction's net energy difference.
4. A useful laboratory result answers the laboratory question. Claims about a product in people require additional, different evidence.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

## Classroom conversation

What could a faster laboratory reaction tell you—and what would it not tell you about wound care?

**Activity:** Draw two hypothetical energy diagrams with the same reactant and product levels but different activation barriers. Then design a paper-only controlled comparison: manipulated variable, responding variable, controls and repeats. Identify one extra evidence question that a health-product claim would raise.

**Teacher note:** The diagrams model general catalysis, not NanoTess's proprietary mechanism. Do not infer clinical effectiveness, patient suitability or regulatory authorization from this activity. No product testing on skin or medical advice.

### Materials and formulation research

Investigate how a material's composition and structure relate to what it does.

- Define a property that matters for the intended use.
- Compare candidate materials using a stated method.
- Explain trade-offs and what evidence is still missing.

**Works with:** Laboratory staff, Manufacturing teams, Application specialists.

**Related routes in this draft:** SAIT — Chemical Laboratory Technology; University of Calgary — Chemistry or Biochemistry. These are connections to explore, not employer hiring requirements.

### Laboratory testing and analysis

Produce measurements that are useful because samples and comparisons are controlled.

- Label and prepare samples so their identity stays traceable.
- Change one planned factor while holding comparison conditions steady.
- Report results with units, repeats and limitations.

**Works with:** Scientists, Quality teams, Process engineers.

**Classroom connection:** Fair comparisons are more informative than an isolated result.

**Related routes in this draft:** SAIT — Chemical Laboratory Technology; University of Calgary — Chemistry or Biochemistry. These are connections to explore, not employer hiring requirements.

### Quality documentation and verification

Keep the chain from a test or batch to a decision clear enough to inspect.

- Check which version of a method and sample was used.
- Review whether measurements support the stated acceptance criteria.
- Record a deviation and the evidence needed before releasing work.

**Works with:** Operations, Scientists, Customer support.

**Classroom connection:** A clear record makes a result checkable by someone who was not there.

**Related routes in this draft:** SAIT — Chemical Laboratory Technology. These are connections to explore, not employer hiring requirements.
