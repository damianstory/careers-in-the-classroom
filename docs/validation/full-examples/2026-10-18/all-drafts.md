# CGY-061-FULL v1 — Standen’s (IMT)

**Full-copy decision: pending. Company proposal approved 18 October 2026. No implementation authorized.**

## Card and introduction

**Why can a steel spring bend repeatedly without staying bent?**

A spring has to move under a load and still be ready for the next one.

**Calgary connection:** Calgary manufacturing facility stated on current company homepage.

**Proposed course:** Physics 20 — D1 — oscillatory motion and restoring force

## Company

IMT Standen’s manufactures leaf springs and suspension components in Calgary. Its product information describes modelling, analysis and controlled heat treatment as part of its manufacturing work. The useful classroom problem is the balance between flexibility and a component that keeps its shape. This is a manufacturing story; a 2026 announcement places distribution through a UAP partnership.

**Problem:** Why can a steel spring bend repeatedly without staying bent?

**Offering:** Leaf springs and suspension components

**Who this serves:** Vehicle and equipment makers, service businesses and people who depend on suspension components.

**Location boundary:** manufacturing. Calgary manufacturing facility stated on current company homepage.

**Original company/work sources:** [CGY-061-S01](https://standens.com/) · [CGY-061-S02](https://standens.com/leaf-springs/) · [CGY-061-S03](https://standens.com/standens-ordering-and-service-announcement/) · [CGY-061-S04](https://standens.com/careers/)

## How it works

1. A load bends a spring away from its unloaded shape. The spring exerts a restoring force.
2. In an ideal elastic model, increasing deflection increases restoring force. A real component has a range over which that model is useful.
3. Material, geometry and manufacturing choices affect performance. Standen’s describes modelling and heat-treatment methods; the illustration is not its production specification.
4. Testing compares the component with its intended duty. A spring that remains deformed after unloading raises a different question from one that returns to its starting shape.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

## Classroom conversation

Would the stiffest possible spring always be the best suspension spring?

**Activity:** Use an invented table: load 0, 100, 200, 300 N; deflection 0, 2, 4, 6 mm. Plot load against deflection, calculate the model stiffness, and predict 400 N only within the assumed elastic range. A second sample retains 1 mm deflection after unloading: discuss why the same model may no longer describe it.

**Teacher note:** The numbers are invented teaching data, not Standen’s test results. A leaf-spring assembly is not necessarily linear at every load. Spring stiffness, damping and material strength are different properties.

## How it works

1. Stored grain and associated organisms can remain biologically active. Temperature, moisture and ventilation influence conditions in storage.
2. Respiration releases energy and can produce CO2. OPI uses CO2 measurements as an indicator alongside temperature and moisture.
3. A measurement describes the air at a sensor and a moment in time. Airflow and placement affect what that reading can reveal.
4. A changing pattern can prompt inspection or a management decision. Several measurements together are more informative than treating one CO2 value as proof of a particular problem.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

**Teacher note:** Respiration is the biological connection; this is not a bin-management guide. Do not claim that all CO2 comes from mould or that a stable temperature proves grain is unaffected. No grain-bin entry activity.

## How it works

1. Start with a testable question: does adding a candidate material change the rate of a defined reaction?
2. Compare otherwise similar systems with and without that material, using a method that measures change over time.
3. A catalyst can provide an alternative reaction pathway. It does not supply unlimited energy or change the reaction's net energy difference.
4. A useful laboratory result answers the laboratory question. Claims about a product in people require additional, different evidence.

## How it works

3. That temperature difference supports a voltage across thermoelectric elements; an attached circuit can supply DC power to a load.
4. Only part of the supplied energy becomes useful electrical output. The rest must still be accounted for, including heat released to the surroundings.

## How it works

1. Start with the task: which deposit or material needs to be affected, and which surrounding materials should be preserved?
2. Specify the solution and test conditions. Acid strength, concentration and a formulation's overall behaviour are different ideas.
3. Compare performance under the same conditions and record more than one outcome, such as the target effect and unwanted material change.
4. Choose a formulation against the whole task. A single pH reading or fast reaction does not describe every handling, corrosion or environmental property.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

## Classroom conversation

Could two solutions with the same pH still require different evidence before choosing between them?

**Activity:** Use paper-only fictional data for formulations A and B: both meet a target-removal criterion, but one changes an equipment-material sample more. Ask students to identify controlled variables, the missing repeat measurements and a second decision criterion. Do not provide a recipe.
