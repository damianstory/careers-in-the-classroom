## How it works

1. A refrigerated space receives heat from its surroundings, incoming goods and everyday activity.
2. Insulation and careful door/handling practices affect how quickly heat enters.
3. Refrigeration transfers heat out of the cooled space and rejects it elsewhere, using energy to do so.
4. People coordinate product movements, measurements and equipment maintenance. A recorded room temperature is only one piece of evidence about a product's journey.

The explanations above are a classroom model grounded in the company/work sources and curriculum source, not a proprietary process specification.

## Classroom conversation

Would a cold room reading prove that every product inside had been handled correctly?

**Activity:** Compare two fictional warehouse plans: one leaves a loading door open continuously; the other groups transfers into short windows. Identify likely heat pathways, an operational trade-off, and measurements needed before predicting energy savings.

**Teacher note:** No food-storage temperatures or safe holding times are prescribed. Refrigeration moves heat; it does not destroy energy. Room-air, product-core and transport temperatures should not be treated as interchangeable.

Receive, locate and move goods while keeping identity and handling requirements visible.

### Refrigeration and air-conditioning mechanic

Install, maintain and troubleshoot equipment that moves heat and conditions air.

- Use drawings to locate components and controls.
- Check operating conditions and investigate a fault.
- Repair or adjust equipment and verify the result.

**Works with:** Controls technicians, Operators, Equipment designers.

**Classroom connection:** A refrigeration system moves heat; it does not create cold as a substance.

**Related routes in this draft:** SAIT — Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Mechanic. These are connections to explore, not employer hiring requirements.

### Dispatch and logistics coordination

Keep materials moving in the right order, with the information needed at each handoff.

- Match deliveries or orders with available capacity.
- Check timing, identification and handling requirements.
- Respond to a delay without losing track of the product or customer need.

**Works with:** Drivers, Warehouse teams, Customers.

**Classroom connection:** A technically sound product can still fail its purpose if timing or handling goes wrong.
