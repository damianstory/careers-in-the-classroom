# OzoneBio

CGY-199-FULL v1 · 2026-10-23

## Classroom question

How can wood waste become a starting material for making nylon?

## Company and Calgary connection

OzoneBio describes a route from lignin-rich waste through pyrolysis and catalytic conversion to chemical building blocks, including adipic acid used in nylon production.

OzoneBio’s website lists an address at Calgary’s Life Sciences Innovation Hub.

## How it works

1. Identify lignin-rich material in a waste feedstock.
2. Use pyrolysis to produce intermediate materials from the biomass.
3. Convert intermediates into useful chemical building blocks.
4. Evaluate those building blocks for further materials manufacturing, including nylon production.

## Classroom activity

Use coloured counters to trace carbon atoms from a fictional biomass feedstock into products and other output streams. Add an adipic-acid building-block card and discuss why making a monomer is different from making the finished polymer. Identify which measurements would be needed before claiming a lower environmental impact.

**Discuss:** Why does a renewable source of carbon not, by itself, prove that a product has zero emissions?

**Limits:** This sequence follows the company’s current technology description. It is not a reaction recipe or a claim of commercial production scale. Company-reported emissions, material performance and biobased-content figures are not reproduced as independently verified results. Adipic acid is a building block, not finished nylon.

## Related work and pathways

- Process engineering: Trace material inputs, conversion stages and product requirements when developing a chemical process.
- Laboratory analysis: Check sample composition and properties to investigate whether a material meets a defined requirement.
