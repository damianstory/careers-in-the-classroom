# VistaVu Solutions

CGY-203-FULL v1 · 2026-10-23

## Classroom question

How can an order, inventory record and invoice stay connected?

## Company and Calgary connection

VistaVu implements connected business systems. Its Resolv extension supports distribution and warehouse processes, while FieldVu connects field tickets with invoicing.

VistaVu lists a Calgary office. Product capabilities are company-wide and are not attributed to a specific Calgary employee or team.

## How it works

1. Give each order, customer and product a stable identifier.
2. Connect order lines to the appropriate order and product.
3. Record fulfilment and invoicing without overwriting the original order.
4. Investigate missing links and discrepancies between ordered, shipped and billed quantities.

## Classroom activity

Build fictional Orders, Products and Order Lines tables for a school-supply store. One order contains two products and one product appears in several orders. Add a shipment and invoice, then test an unknown product ID and a partial shipment. Explain why the records should remain related but distinct.

**Discuss:** Why is one giant spreadsheet row a poor way to represent an order containing several products?

**Limits:** This is a simplified relational model, not VistaVu’s actual database schema. No customer information, billing advice or promise of automatic error prevention is included.

## Course connection

INF2070 · Database 2: selected outcomes 1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 2.4, 2.8, 3.4. Related tables, identifiers and referential integrity. The company activity is an editorial application.

## Related work and pathways

- Software and data: Connect orders, item records and invoices using clear relationships and validation checks.
- Technical application support: Trace a reported mismatch across connected records and help users explain the problem.

- SAIT — Software Development: Learn to design, test and build software across front-end and back-end systems. Check the provider’s current prerequisites and intake availability. This is a related learning route, not a hiring promise or an employer requirement.
