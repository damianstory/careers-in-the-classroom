# Role copy — 20 October 2026
Version 1 · pending final-copy review. Generic occupational copy is an educational description, not evidence of a particular employee or vacancy.

## Animal care and welfare — `animal-care`

Support animals day to day and notice changes that matter.

- **Provide daily care:** Follow prescribed diets and maintain clean, suitable habitats.
- **Observe and record:** Track behaviour, weight and body condition, and report changes to the team.
- **Work with specialists:** Support veterinary procedures and enrichment under established welfare and safety protocols.

Collaborators: Veterinarians, Conservation researchers, Animal care assistants, Facility staff.
Classroom connection: Individual health and behaviour influence survival. Daily observations provide evidence, while population outcomes require follow-up beyond care.

## Ecommerce operations coordinator — `new-ecommerce-operations`

Connect online customer choices with reliable order and delivery processes.

- **Clarify:** Make product and purchase options understandable.
- **Coordinate:** Connect orders, inventory and fulfilment information.
- **Improve:** Review customer needs and process problems using evidence.

Collaborators: Marketing colleagues, Warehouse teams, Customer support.
Classroom connection: Different purchase models create different customer benefits and delivery requirements.

## Event operations coordinator — `new-event-operations`

Organize the people, services and practical details that make an event work.

- **Plan:** Connect visitor needs with a workable event layout.
- **Coordinate:** Arrange schedules and dependencies across teams.
- **Adapt:** Compare delivery conditions with the plan and resolve conflicts.

Collaborators: Venue teams, Service suppliers, Visitor-support teams.
Classroom connection: An event turns a value proposition into a network of resource decisions.

## Financial analyst — `new-financial-analysis`

Compare financial information and explain what the numbers can and cannot establish.

- **Define:** Check the period, scope and meaning of each measure.
- **Calculate:** Compare statements or scenarios using consistent definitions.
- **Explain:** Describe the result, assumptions and important omissions.

Collaborators: Accounting teams, Operations teams.
Classroom connection: Ratios and changes are useful only when their underlying quantities are comparable.

## Franchise support coordinator — `new-franchise-support`

Help local operators apply shared systems and communicate operational needs.

- **Explain:** Help operators understand available systems and support.
- **Coordinate:** Connect training and operating questions with the right team.
- **Learn:** Gather feedback about where support or processes need improvement.

Collaborators: Local operators, Training teams, Marketing colleagues.
Classroom connection: A franchise model divides decisions and resources across connected businesses.

## Plant biotechnology scientist — `new-plant-biotechnology`

Investigate how plant characteristics relate to biological information and growing conditions.

- **Frame:** Define a trait and a testable research question.
- **Compare:** Plan controlled comparisons and record observations.
- **Interpret:** Separate evidence for a trait from effects of growing conditions.

Collaborators: Laboratory technicians, Crop-development teams.
Classroom connection: Genetic information and controlled comparisons connect laboratory questions to crop traits.

## Procurement specialist — `new-procurement`

Help an organization choose products and coordinate what it buys.

- **Specify:** Understand the products and quantities the business needs.
- **Compare:** Evaluate options against relevant requirements and constraints.
- **Coordinate:** Connect purchasing decisions with inventory and delivery planning.

Collaborators: Suppliers, Warehouse teams, Sales teams.
Classroom connection: Customer needs become purchasing requirements and operational trade-offs.

## Retail operations coordinator — `new-retail-operations`

Connect day-to-day service with the resources a retail business needs.

- **Observe:** Identify customer needs and service constraints.
- **Coordinate:** Organize tasks and resources to deliver the service.
- **Review:** Compare outcomes and trade-offs before changing a process.

Collaborators: Store teams, Customer-service teams, Finance colleagues.
Classroom connection: Customer benefit and operating costs need to be considered together.

## Science educator — `new-science-education`

Help people understand and apply scientific ideas through explanations and practical learning.

- **Understand:** Identify the learner’s question and context.
- **Explain:** Choose a model or demonstration that makes the idea visible.
- **Check:** Ask learners to apply the idea and discuss the model’s limits.

Collaborators: Technical specialists, Community partners.
Classroom connection: A good explanation connects a concept to a decision and makes uncertainty visible.

## Sport physiologist — `new-sport-physiology`

Use knowledge of the body and measured evidence to help interpret training responses.

- **Plan:** Connect a measurement to a specific training question.
- **Measure:** Use consistent conditions and record relevant context.
- **Interpret:** Discuss patterns and limitations with coaches and athletes.

Collaborators: Coaches, Athletes, Other sport-science professionals.
Classroom connection: Respiratory and circulatory systems help explain responses to exercise.

## Bioprocess development — `work-bioprocess`

Develop repeatable ways to make and purify biological materials. This work connects molecular science to manufacturing conditions and checks whether a change improves the process.

- **Investigate:** Define a comparison between process conditions.
- **Test and compare:** Track production and purification steps for each batch.
- **Explain and improve:** Use analytical results to decide which change to test next.

Collaborators: Molecular scientists, analytical staff and quality teams.
Classroom connection: Knowing a sequence or molecule is different from producing a reliable batch of it.

## Instrumentation and controls — `work-controls`

Keep a process measurable and responsive. Controls work connects sensors, decisions and actuators so the system can respond when conditions change.

- **Investigate:** Check whether sensors are reading reliably.
- **Test and compare:** Compare measurements with the intended operating range.
- **Explain and improve:** Test how the system responds to a change or fault.

Collaborators: Operators, electricians and process engineers.
Classroom connection: Feedback uses a measured result to inform the next action.

## Electrician — `work-electrician`

Build and check electrical installations using drawings, standards and trade procedures.

- **Read:** Interpret drawings and identify the intended circuit connections.
- **Install:** Fit and connect components using approved methods.
- **Verify:** Carry out permitted inspections and tests and document issues.

Collaborators: Controls technologists, Other trades, Inspectors.
Classroom connection: A circuit diagram describes intended behaviour; installation and verification connect that model to a real system.

## Instrument development and testing — `work-instrument`

Help build and check the equipment that produces measurements. Instrument work joins hardware, software and experiments rather than treating a reading as automatically correct.

- **Investigate:** Check performance using a known reference.
- **Test and compare:** Investigate drift, noise or inconsistent readings.
- **Explain and improve:** Document tests so later users know what the instrument can establish.

Collaborators: Scientists, electronics specialists and software developers.
Classroom connection: Measurement uncertainty is part of using a tool responsibly.

## Laboratory analysis — `work-lab`

Make measurements that other people can trust. A laboratory worker prepares samples, uses a method consistently and records enough detail for someone else to interpret the result.

- **Investigate:** Prepare and label samples so their identity is preserved.
- **Test and compare:** Run comparisons or reference samples to check the method.
- **Explain and improve:** Record results, units and limitations before sharing findings.

Collaborators: Scientists, process engineers and quality teams.
Classroom connection: A repeatable method and a fair comparison matter as much as a single impressive number.

## Dispatch and logistics coordination — `work-logistics`

Keep materials moving in the right order, with the information needed at each handoff.

- **Match:** Match deliveries or orders with available capacity.
- **Check:** Check timing, identification and handling requirements.
- **Respond:** Respond to a delay without losing track of the product or customer need.

Collaborators: Drivers, Warehouse teams, Customers.
Classroom connection: A technically sound product can still fail its purpose if timing or handling goes wrong.

## Machining and dimensional inspection — `work-machining`

Make parts fit their intended purpose by controlling dimensions. Machining combines tools, setup and measurement; finishing a part is not the same as proving it meets its specification.

- **Investigate:** Interpret a drawing and plan a sequence of cuts.
- **Test and compare:** Set up the machine and check the workpiece.
- **Explain and improve:** Measure critical dimensions and document deviations.

Collaborators: Designers, production teams and quality inspectors.
Classroom connection: Tolerance describes an acceptable range rather than a perfectly exact number.

## Manufacturing and quality work — `work-manufacturing`

Make repeatable components and check that the output matches a specification.

- **Read:** Read a drawing or work instruction before setting up a task.
- **Measure:** Measure key dimensions or properties and record the result.
- **Investigate:** Investigate variation rather than treating every difference as acceptable.

Collaborators: Design teams, Machine operators, Quality staff.
Classroom connection: Precision, tolerances and repeatable methods connect measurement to useful products.

## Materials research and testing — `work-materials`

Investigate whether a material improves a product for a defined purpose. A useful comparison separates the effect of the added material from other changes in the test.

- **Investigate:** Prepare comparable samples with and without an additive.
- **Test and compare:** Measure a relevant property under consistent conditions.
- **Explain and improve:** Explain performance changes alongside uncertainty and practical constraints.

Collaborators: Chemists, process engineers and product developers.
Classroom connection: Stronger, lighter and more conductive are different properties requiring different tests.

## Mechanical design and prototyping — `work-mechanical`

Translate a need into a component or machine that can be built and tested. Designers use dimensions, material properties and constraints to evaluate alternatives.

- **Investigate:** Create and revise drawings or a digital model.
- **Test and compare:** Choose materials and dimensions for the intended conditions.
- **Explain and improve:** Compare the prototype with its requirements and refine the design.

Collaborators: Machinists, fabricators, test staff and customers.
Classroom connection: Geometry, forces and material properties shape real design trade-offs.

## Industrial machinery assembly and maintenance — `work-millwright`

Make machinery work together as a physical system. Millwright work connects assembly, alignment, inspection and troubleshooting.

- **Investigate:** Read equipment drawings and plan assembly steps.
- **Test and compare:** Align and connect mechanical components.
- **Explain and improve:** Check operation and record problems that need adjustment.

Collaborators: Designers, operators, electricians and fabrication teams.
Classroom connection: Small alignment errors can change how forces and motion pass through a machine.

## Power-electronics design — `work-power`

Control how electrical energy moves between components. Power-electronics work combines circuit design with testing, thermal limits and the behaviour of the complete system.

- **Investigate:** Choose and document a circuit design.
- **Test and compare:** Test its response across operating conditions.
- **Explain and improve:** Investigate losses and component limits before revising the design.

Collaborators: Electrical technologists, firmware developers and manufacturing teams.
Classroom connection: Power describes the rate of energy transfer; stored energy describes how much is available.

## Quality and traceability — `work-quality`

Make it possible to explain how a result or product was produced. Quality work checks records, methods and acceptance criteria so variation can be investigated rather than hidden.

- **Investigate:** Check that materials and samples have traceable identities.
- **Test and compare:** Compare recorded results with defined acceptance criteria.
- **Explain and improve:** Investigate deviations and document what changed.

Collaborators: Production, laboratory, engineering and customer teams.
Classroom connection: Repeated results need both measurements and records of the conditions that produced them.

## Software and data systems — `work-software`

Build the tools that move, process and display information. Reliable software needs testing against missing, delayed and unexpected data as well as ideal examples.

- **Investigate:** Translate a user need into a testable behaviour.
- **Test and compare:** Build and test a data-processing or display feature.
- **Explain and improve:** Investigate failures and document assumptions about inputs.

Collaborators: Domain experts, designers, users and hardware teams.
Classroom connection: An algorithm’s output depends on the quality and meaning of its inputs.

## Spatial data and mapping — `work-spatial`

Turn measurements tied to places into usable spatial information. The work involves coordinates, scale and checking that datasets describe the same location and time.

- **Investigate:** Check coordinate systems and measurement dates.
- **Test and compare:** Align and inspect spatial datasets.
- **Explain and improve:** Present maps or models with their limitations.

Collaborators: Surveyors, engineers, field operators and software teams.
Classroom connection: Models can be detailed while still containing gaps or misaligned measurements.

## Technical application support — `work-technical-support`

Help people use a technical product in the conditions of their own workplace.

- **Ask:** Ask what the user is trying to measure or control.
- **Separate:** Separate a setup problem from a sensor or process problem.
- **Explain:** Explain the next diagnostic step and record what changed.

Collaborators: Customers, Product developers, Field technicians.
Classroom connection: Troubleshooting means testing explanations one at a time.
