# AgGene
CGY-103-FULL · version 1 · **final copy pending** · proposal approved 20 October 2026

## Question

How can a plant trait help a crop cope with heat or drought?

Changing a crop starts with deciding which trait would help it in the field.

## About the organization

AgGene researches plant traits, including responses to heat and drought and crop quality.

Who it serves: Crop developers and growers facing changing growing conditions.

Calgary connection: AgGene works from Calgary’s Life Sciences Innovation Hub at University Innovation Quarter.

Company presence and company-wide work are independent claims. Do not present every role or project as Calgary-based.

## How it works

1. Define the trait: decide which plant characteristic would address a growing problem.
2. Investigate the biology: researchers study how genetic information relates to the characteristic.
3. Compare plants: controlled comparisons help separate a trait from differences in growing conditions.
4. Test the limits: a promising laboratory result still needs evidence in relevant growing environments.

## Classroom use

**Try it:** Compare a fictional two-by-two table of plant varieties under watered and dry conditions. Identify the comparison that tests the trait and one variable that must be controlled.
**Discuss:** Why would testing only the strongest-looking plant give an unreliable answer?
**Teacher note:** The local innovation-hub source explicitly describes gene editing. Do not present editing as identical to selective breeding, or describe every edit as inserting foreign DNA. Field trials are not established as Calgary activities.

**Curriculum:** Biology 30; 30–C3.2k. verified genetic-information anchor; gene editing and drought-trait experiments are extensions
