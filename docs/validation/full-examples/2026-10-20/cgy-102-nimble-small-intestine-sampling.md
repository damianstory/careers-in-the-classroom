# Nimble Science
CGY-102-FULL · version 1 · **final copy pending** · proposal approved 20 October 2026

## Question

Why might a stool sample miss what is happening in the small intestine?

Where a sample comes from changes what it can tell you.

## About the organization

Nimble develops the SIMBA platform for collecting intestinal samples in research.

Who it serves: Researchers studying the small intestine and its contents.

Calgary connection: Nimble lists a Calgary location. Its earlier Calgary community study is historical, not a current recruitment notice.

Company presence and company-wide work are independent claims. Do not present every role or project as Calgary-based.

## How it works

1. Locate the question: the small intestine and large intestine are different sampling environments.
2. Reach the location: an ingestible device is designed to collect fluid from within the gastrointestinal tract.
3. Preserve the sample: collection and sealing help protect information for later analysis.
4. Interpret cautiously: a sample describes a place and time; it does not describe every part of the digestive tract.

## Classroom use

**Try it:** Use a fictional diagram with three sampling locations and different coloured tokens. Compare the conclusions supported by each sample and identify one missing piece of evidence.
**Discuss:** When can an easier-to-collect sample be a poor substitute for the sample you actually need?
**Teacher note:** SIMBA is investigational and used within authorized studies. The historic Calgary study does not establish current recruitment or routine clinical availability. Never ask students to collect biological samples.
