# Calgary Wildlife Rehabilitation Society
CGY-108-FULL · version 1 · **final copy pending** · proposal approved 20 October 2026

## Question

What evidence shows that an injured wild animal is ready for release?

Recovery has to be judged against the challenges an animal will face outside care.

## About the organization

Calgary Wildlife rehabilitates injured and orphaned wildlife and releases animals when appropriate.

Who it serves: Wild animals and the community that encounters them.

Calgary connection: Calgary Wildlife operates a rehabilitation centre in Calgary.

Company presence and company-wide work are independent claims. Do not present every role or project as Calgary-based.

## How it works

1. Understand the problem: staff assess an animal’s condition and care needs.
2. Record recovery: observations over time are more informative than a single encouraging moment.
3. Consider life outside care: species needs and habitat conditions shape the questions to ask.
4. Make an evidence-based decision: qualified staff decide whether release is appropriate; a classroom checklist cannot replace that judgement.

## Classroom use

**Try it:** Give groups a fictional patient observation record and habitat description. Ask what evidence is still missing before a professional could consider release; do not ask students to declare an animal releasable.
**Discuss:** Why is surviving in a sheltered enclosure different from coping in a habitat?
**Teacher note:** Biology 20 habitat and limiting-factor concepts are an extension to individual rehabilitation. The reviewed sources document rehabilitation and release stories but not a complete release-assessment protocol.

**Curriculum:** Biology 20; 20–B1.2k; 20–B1.4k. verified habitat/limiting-factor anchor; rehabilitation is an extension
