CGY-101-FULL · version 1 · **final copy pending** · proposal approved 20 October 2026

## Question

How can an RNA message be delivered without being destroyed first?

An RNA instruction is useful only if it reaches the cells that can read it.

## About the organization

Providence develops mRNA medicines and lipid nanoparticle delivery formulations.

Who it serves: Drug-development teams investigating new treatments.

Calgary connection: Providence lists operations in Calgary and Toronto, including a Calgary contact address.

Company presence and company-wide work are independent claims. Do not present every role or project as Calgary-based.

## How it works

1. Choose the message: an RNA sequence carries instructions for making a protein.
2. Protect the cargo: a lipid formulation packages the RNA for delivery.
3. Compare formulations: researchers investigate stability, delivery and unwanted effects rather than assuming one carrier suits every task.
4. Read the evidence: delivery is one stage in development, not proof that a treatment is safe or effective.

## Classroom use

**Try it:** Use paper messages and different envelopes as a delivery analogy. List what the model represents and what it cannot explain about cells, uptake or immune responses.
**Discuss:** Why might a carrier that protects its cargo very well still fail to deliver it?
**Teacher note:** The RNA-to-protein connection is curricular; nanoparticle delivery is an extension. The envelope model is an analogy, not a laboratory protocol. Describe development, not approved treatment. Calgary and Toronto operations are verified; do not assign a particular experiment to Calgary.
