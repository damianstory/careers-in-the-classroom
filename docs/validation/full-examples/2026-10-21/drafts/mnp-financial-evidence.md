## How can checking evidence make a set of financial statements more trustworthy?

MNP provides accounting, assurance and advisory services. Its Calgary office lists these services, and a Calgary-based specialist describes work on internal controls over financial reporting.

**Calgary connection:** MNP's original Calgary office page and a local professional profile both support a substantive Calgary assurance and financial-controls connection.

## How it works

1. Identify the financial statement or process assertion that needs support.
2. Select relevant records and understand how they were produced.
3. Compare the evidence with the assertion and investigate inconsistencies.
4. Document the conclusion and limitations rather than assuming every discrepancy is fraud.

## Classroom connection

**Financial Analysis** — selected scope: 1.2, 1.3

**Try it:** Review a fictional sales summary alongside invoices and a delivery record. Identify an unexplained mismatch, propose a follow-up question and distinguish checking evidence from issuing an audit opinion.

**Discuss:** What evidence would change your confidence in the reported total?

**Model limits:** A classroom record check is not an audit or professional assurance engagement. CPA education, examination and practical-experience requirements are separate from an introductory diploma.

### Assurance accountant

Examine financial records and controls to assess the support for reported information.

**At this organization:** Examine records and controls, investigate differences and document support for financial-reporting conclusions.

- **Understand:** Identify the statement or process being examined.
- **Investigate:** Compare relevant records and follow up unexplained differences.
- **Document:** Record the evidence, conclusion and important limitations.

**Collaborators:** Finance teams, Operations staff, Other assurance specialists

**Classroom connection:** A reported number needs evidence; finding a mismatch is the start of an investigation, not proof of fraud.
