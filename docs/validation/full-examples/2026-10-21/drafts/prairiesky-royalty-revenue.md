# PrairieSky Royalty

CGY-173-FULL · version 1 · final-copy decision: **pending**

## How can a company earn revenue from resources without drilling the wells itself?

PrairieSky Royalty holds mineral and royalty interests. The company describes earning royalties from production by others rather than operating the wells itself.

**Calgary connection:** PrairieSky's investor material states that its staff work from its Calgary head office.

## How it works

1. Distinguish the rights held by the royalty company from the operator's production work.
2. Identify the production, price and contract information relevant to a payment.
3. Compare royalty revenue and expenses over a consistent reporting period.
4. Explain what a change in revenue does and does not reveal about the business.

## Classroom connection

**Financial Analysis** — selected scope: 1.2, 1.3, 2.2, 2.4

**Try it:** Use a fictional royalty model with 100 units sold at $50 and a 5% rate. Calculate $250, then vary price or volume separately. Label all values as invented and discuss omitted contract terms and costs.

**Discuss:** How could revenue change even if this company did not drill a new well?

**Model limits:** The 5% example is invented, not PrairieSky's average or a real agreement. The exercise is not an investment recommendation. It does not convert executive career histories into current analyst vacancies.

## Roles and learning pathways

### Financial analyst

Compare financial information and explain what the numbers can and cannot establish.

**At this organization:** Compare revenue and expenses, reconcile definitions and explain the assumptions behind a change.

- **Define:** Check the period, scope and meaning of each measure.
- **Calculate:** Compare statements or scenarios using consistent definitions.
- **Explain:** Describe the result, assumptions and important omissions.

**Collaborators:** Accounting teams, Operations teams

**Classroom connection:** Ratios and changes are useful only when their underlying quantities are comparable.
