## Why can more busy days change revenue even when a daily price barely changes?

Precision Drilling provides drilling services and reports activity and financial measures. Its 2025 results discuss utilization days and revenue per utilization day.

**Calgary connection:** The company identifies Calgary corporate operations. Its drilling activity occurs across multiple regions; the classroom story focuses on interpreting business records.

## How it works

1. Define the period, region and activity measure being compared.
2. Examine how many days equipment was used and the revenue associated with that activity.
3. Separate the effects of activity and rate changes in a simplified model.
4. Compare the model with the reporting definitions and identify what it leaves out.

## Classroom connection

**Financial Analysis** — selected scope: 1.2, 1.3, 2.2, 2.4

**Try it:** For a fictional service, compare 100 days at $1,000 per day with 120 days at $950 per day. Calculate $100,000 versus $114,000 and explain why revenue rises while the rate falls.

**Discuss:** What costs and other revenue items would you need before discussing profit?

**Model limits:** The values are invented Canadian-dollar figures. Days multiplied by rate is a teaching model, not a reconstruction of Precision's full revenue. The cited 2025 announcement is explicitly unaudited.

## Roles and learning pathways

### Financial analyst

Compare financial information and explain what the numbers can and cannot establish.

**At this organization:** Check periods and units, compare activity with financial results and explain model limitations.

- **Define:** Check the period, scope and meaning of each measure.
- **Calculate:** Compare statements or scenarios using consistent definitions.
- **Explain:** Describe the result, assumptions and important omissions.

**Collaborators:** Accounting teams, Operations teams

**Classroom connection:** Ratios and changes are useful only when their underlying quantities are comparable.
